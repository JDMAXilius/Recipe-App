# Segmented controls
URL: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls
## Summary
A segmented control is a row of two or more equal-width segments acting as buttons, usually for a single choice among closely related options. In iOS it switches related subviews; separate app sections belong in a tab bar.
## Best practices
- Use for closely related choices affecting an object, state or view.
- Good when grouping and visible selection state matter.
- Don't mix action segments and selection segments in one control.
- Limit segments: about five to seven on wide layouts, about five on iPhone.
- Keep segment widths, and icon/title widths, consistent.
- Use text or images, not both, in one control.
- Use similarly sized content in each segment.
- Labels: nouns or noun phrases, title case; no introductory text needed.
## iOS specifics
- Use to switch between closely related subviews (Calendar's New Event sheet: Event / Reminder).
- For completely separate sections, use a tab bar instead.
## Specs & numbers
- Minimum segments: two.
- Max: about 5–7 wide; about 5 on iPhone.
## New in iOS 26 / Liquid Glass
Not mentioned on this page.
## Do / Don't
- Do: "Ingredients | Steps | Nutrition" text segments, equal width. / Don't: one icon segment mixed with two text segments.
- Do: 3 segments on iPhone. / Don't: 8 cramped segments.
- Do: segments switch subviews in one screen. / Don't: segmented control as app-level navigation instead of tabs.
- Do: all segments selection-based. / Don't: two selectable segments plus a "Share" action segment.
- Do: noun labels "Day | Week | Month". / Don't: verb labels "Show Day | View Week".
