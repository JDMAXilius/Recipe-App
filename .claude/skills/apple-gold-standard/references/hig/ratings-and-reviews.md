# Ratings and reviews
URL: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ratings-and-reviews

## Summary
People check ratings before downloading, so a great experience and good timing matter. Ask only after real engagement, at natural pauses, rarely, and with the system-provided prompt.

## Best practices
- Time requests based on signals like launch count/frequency, features explored, or tasks completed.
- Ask only after demonstrated engagement (e.g., completing a level or significant task).
- Never ask on first launch or during onboarding; early asks can invite negative reviews.
- Don't interrupt an active task or gameplay; use natural stopping points.
- Don't pester; wait at least a week or two and look for more engagement before asking again.
- Prefer the system prompt (RequestReviewAction): it checks for prior feedback, offers a rating plus optional review, and is dismissible in one tap.
- People can opt out of these prompts for all apps.
- Weigh resetting the summary rating on a new version: more current, but fewer ratings may deter downloads.
- Remember people can always rate in the App Store.

## Do / Don't
- Do: Show the system rating prompt after someone finishes cooking their third recipe. / Don't: Ask for a rating on first launch.
- Do: Prompt on the completion screen after a task. / Don't: Pop the prompt mid-step while someone follows a recipe.
- Do: Use the native star-rating system prompt. / Don't: A custom "Do you love us? Yes/No" gate that routes only happy users to the App Store.
- Do: Wait weeks before asking again. / Don't: Ask every session after dismissal.
- Do: Let onboarding finish uninterrupted. / Don't: Put a rating request inside the onboarding flow.
