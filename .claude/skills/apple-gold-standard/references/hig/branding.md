# Branding
URL: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/branding

## Summary
Express brand identity in ways that are recognizable yet feel native to the platform. Branding should come through voice, judicious accent color, typography, and content, while always deferring to content and using familiar components and patterns.

## Best practices
- Use a consistent brand voice and tone in all copy (e.g., plain words, occasional exclamation marks and emoji for an upbeat brand).
- Apply the accent color judiciously: limit it on controls; use it for primary actions and status (unread badges, selected tab icon).
- Move brand color into the content layer, where it scrolls under Liquid Glass and gets picked up dynamically.
- A custom font is fine if legible at all sizes and supports Bold Text and Dynamic Type; good pattern: custom font for headlines/subheads, system font for body and captions.
- Express brand through familiar components; when customizing, preserve standard sizing, placement, and behavior.
- Branding always defers to content; avoid screen space spent only on brand assets.
- Use standard patterns consistently: UI in expected places, standard symbols for common actions, established navigation and modality.
- Don't sprinkle your logo throughout the app unless it gives needed context.
- Don't use the launch screen for branding; put brand content in a welcome/onboarding screen instead.
- Follow Apple trademark guidelines; no Apple trademarks in app name or images.

## New in iOS 26 / Liquid Glass
- Keep brand color off most Liquid Glass controls; place it in the content layer so glass picks it up as content scrolls beneath.

## Do / Don't
- Do: brand color on the selected tab icon and the primary "Cook" button. / Don't: brand-tint every button, toggle, and bar.
- Do: colorful brand illustration in content cards scrolling under the glass tab bar. / Don't: a solid brand-color navigation bar.
- Do: custom display font for the screen headline, SF for ingredient list. / Don't: decorative script font for body text.
- Do: a branded welcome screen in onboarding. / Don't: a logo-only splash launch screen.
- Do: standard share icon in the toolbar. / Don't: a custom mascot glyph for "share".
- Do: no logo in the nav bar. / Don't: a persistent logo header eating vertical space on every screen.
