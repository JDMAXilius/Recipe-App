# Group C: Otto's direct competitors (magic-moment study)

Sources checked 2026-11-04. Labels: **seen** (I looked at the image/screen) · **doc** (official store/site text) · **report** (third party) · **inferred**.
Mobbin coverage: only ReciMe has an onboarding flow on Mobbin. Samsung Food, Paprika, Mealime, Ollie and Plan to Eat are not there (also recorded in `docs/history/IMPORT_SHARE_RESEARCH.md`). None of the six has a lane file in `docs/flow-maps/lanes/`.

**Two status flags before you read on:**
- **Mealime has shut down.** Its own page says the service ended on **Oct 21, 2026**. Pro features were made free until then, personal data is deleted, and the recipes moved to "Meals Hub" inside Albertsons grocery apps (doc: https://www.mealime.com/closing). The US App Store page and the iTunes lookup now both return 404 or nothing.
- **Ollie's meal app (id6480014476) is not live in the US store today.** The lookup returns nothing and the store page is a 404. AppGoblin last crawled it on 2026-10-28 under the name "Ollie for Meals: Meal Planning" (★4.77, 951 ratings, updated 2026-09-09). ollie.ai now sells an SMS-only "personal assistant for everyday life" instead. Both points are reports. Its screenshots came from Apple's CDN through AppGoblin's URLs.

## ReciMe (recime) · id1593779280 · ★4.79 (326,674 ratings) · subtitle "Grocery Lists, Prep & Cookbook"
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** "I save recipes from reels and can never find them again, or I have to rewatch the video over and over."
- **Magic moment:** You paste or share an Instagram or TikTok video and get back a clean card with the ingredients and steps, even when the video had no caption.
- **Path to it:** The onboarding has 20 screens. Screen 10 is "Import recipes from anywhere" with a **Try it now** button. Screen 11 is "Add your first recipe", where you pick one of 4 sample blog recipes or "Google a different recipe" (Skip is allowed). So the first real import happens about halfway through onboarding, roughly 60–90 s in. After that come "Organize" screens, then "We offer 7 Days for free", a notification-permission primer, a **trial paywall** ("Choose your trial experience": FREE 7-day trial or 30-day trial for S$0.98, "No Payment Now", a dismiss **X**), and only then **Create account, which is skippable**. The first import is therefore **before the account and before the paywall**. The free tier is metered: the home screen shows a "5/5" lightning import counter. [seen: Mobbin flow https://mobbin.com/flows/cd22af24-931c-4888-9b62-3ee2b058c4bf; 5 imports per week free per our earlier research doc; the time is inferred]
- **How it makes it personal:** The goals and cooking-frequency answers are reflected back as cheerleading ("92% of users report… eat healthier"; a "Scattered recipes → Organized recipes" graph). The really personal part is that it imports *your own* saved social posts. Your mess becomes a tidy cookbook.
- **Store page pitch:** 1 = "World's *most popular* recipe organizer" (blue panel, no phone, "Trusted by 15 million+ Home Cooks" laurel). 2 = "Save recipes from social media" (an Instagram post of a stew ringed by FB, Pinterest, TikTok, IG and YouTube logos). 3 = "All your recipes, in one place" (the My Recipes cookbook grid, with a visible "3 left" import-credit chip). 4 = "Import from your screenshots" (camera framing a handwritten recipe card). [seen] The store also runs an in-app event called **"Magic Meal Plan: Get personalized recipes, tailored for you."** [doc], which suggests ReciMe is moving into planning.
- **Users' words (reviews):** Reviewers say it pulled a recipe out of a video with no caption. One calls it "the app I didn't know I needed". Several say they were about to delete it on seeing the subscription, took the trial anyway, and kept it once they started importing their old saved posts. The 2-star complaints are about hitting the import limit after about 6 saves without having been told about it, and imports that fail.
- **Evidence:** iTunes lookup and RSS mostHelpful (doc); store screenshots `shots/recime/strip.png` (seen); Mobbin onboarding flow (seen); `docs/history/IMPORT_SHARE_RESEARCH.md` (our doc).
- **What Otto can take:** (1) Put a *real* import inside onboarding, using a pre-picked sample or "paste one you saved", so the parse happens before the questionnaire is even finished. (2) Put the paywall *after* that import and make the account skippable until it's needed for sync.

## Samsung Food, ex-Whisk (samsung-food) · id1133637674 · ★4.78 (6,441 ratings) · subtitle "Meal Planner & Recipe Saver"
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** "I have 50 browser tabs open to save recipes, and the sites are buried in ads and pop-ups."
- **Magic moment:** You save a recipe from any site (or sync your Pinterest), tap once, and its exact quantities land in a shopping list sorted by aisle that your partner can see.
- **Path to it:** Not verified. It's not on Mobbin and I found no teardown. The store text says the core is free (save, plan, list, share). **Food+** ($6.99/mo or $59.99/yr per reports) gates the AI-personalized weekly plans, recipe customization, step-by-step Smart Cooking mode and Vision AI pantry scanning. So the paywall sits *beyond* the first moment, as an upsell, not a gate. [doc for the tiering; report for prices: https://stuff.co.za/2024/09/06/samsung-food-upgraded-ai-app-pay-for/; screen count and account position unknown]
- **How it makes it personal:** "Recipe recommendations from what you eat and save" (screenshot 4) is learned over time, not shown on first open. One review says the diet preferences entered at setup are ignored (gluten-free user shown gluten recipes).
- **Store page pitch:** 1 = "Cook Jamie Oliver's Recipes" (photo of Jamie Oliver in a kitchen; celebrity, no UI). 2 = "Effortless Meal Planning" (the Plan tab: This week, a Queue, Today at 1670 Cal). 3 = "4.8 rated · 'This is the best meal planning app I have ever used'" (a checked meal list). 4 = "Recipe recommendations from what you eat and save" (food collage). [seen]
- **Users' words (reviews):** "Where has this been my whole life." Shared and invited grocery lists with a spouse are called life-changing, and one reviewer says the app "saved me hundreds of dollars". Another says syncing Pinterest saves into a list was "a breeze". The dominant complaint: since the Samsung rebrand, full-screen ads cover the directions mid-cook.
- **Evidence:** iTunes lookup, store page, RSS (doc); `shots/samsung-food/strip.png` (seen); stuff.co.za and Android Authority (report).
- **What Otto can take:** (1) Make "recipe → a household list your partner sees update live" a felt beat: show the partner's phone or a "Shared with Ana" badge the moment items land. (2) Otto has no ads. Say so, because it's the #1 thing Samsung Food users are angry about.

## Paprika Recipe Manager 3 (paprika) · id1303222868 · ★4.90 (54,213 ratings) · subtitle "Organize your recipes" · $4.99 one-time
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** "Wading through the blogger's life story to get to the recipe," plus binders and Pinterest boards that can't be searched.
- **Magic moment:** In Paprika's built-in browser you open a cluttered recipe blog, tap save, and everything but the ingredients and steps disappears into your own searchable, offline cookbook.
- **Path to it:** Pay before the first open (a $4.99 **paid upfront** app, no trial). After that there is no account wall (sync is optional) and no onboarding quiz. You open the browser and save a recipe: about 2–3 taps, under a minute. [doc: price and description; the steps are inferred from the store text and long-standing app behaviour, not seen]
- **How it makes it personal:** It's your own recipes, categories, notes, ratings and family names as tags. There's no AI or questionnaire. The personal part comes from the collection growing (reviewers have 2,000–6,000 recipes).
- **Store page pitch:** 1 = "Sync with all your devices" (the All Recipes photo grid with star ratings). 2 = "Save your favorite recipes" (a recipe page with Scale & Convert). 3 = "Create grocery lists" (a list by aisle, each item tagged with its source recipe). 4 = "Make meal plans" (a month calendar with breakfast/lunch/dinner/dessert). These are dated 2019-era red-bar UI shots. [seen]
- **Users' words (reviews):** People say it "changed my life", that it skips the fluff "in one click", and that they bought it again. It's loved for being a *purchase, not a subscription*, for working offline, for being shared with a whole family, and for timers detected in the step text. The complaints are about losing data and having no photo-to-recipe OCR.
- **Evidence:** iTunes lookup and RSS (doc); `shots/paprika/strip.png` (seen).
- **What Otto can take:** (1) In-recipe tappable timers parsed from the step text are a cheap delight that Paprika users single out. (2) Paprika's 4.9 rating is a warning: a recipe-keeper's value only builds over weeks. Otto's paywall can't ask people to pay for a library they haven't built yet, so it has to sell the *plan*, not the box.

## Mealime (mealime) · id1079999103 · ★4.80 (~67K ratings, report) · subtitle n/a (page gone) · **shut down Oct 21, 2026**
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** "What healthy dinner can I cook in 30 minutes this week, and what do I buy?"
- **Magic moment:** You answer a few questions (diet, allergies, dislikes) and see a week of 30-minute dinners built for you, then a grocery list for exactly those meals, sorted by aisle.
- **Path to it:** About **11 onboarding steps** (diet → allergies → dislikes and so on) → plan → list. A **soft, dismissible paywall** appears right after onboarding, about 1 minute in, and the core stays free. The plan and list come about 70–90 s after first open. [report: https://screensdesign.com/showcase/mealime-meal-plans-recipes; help docs say dislikes and allergies are set "during signup": https://support.mealime.com/article/60-choose-your-dislikes (doc)]. Pro was $2.99/mo (doc).
- **How it makes it personal:** Allergies and dislikes are honoured in every recipe. The list is built from *your* picked meals and servings.
- **Store page pitch:** 1 = "Meal planning made easy." (illustrated flat-lay, no UI). 2 = "Healthy recipes" (a Roasted Salmon & Asparagus card, 30 minutes · 4 servings, with a Cookware tab). 3 = "Organized grocery list" (Produce, Dairy and so on, with a "Shop online" button). Only 3 iPhone shots were available. [seen, via AppGoblin's mzstatic URLs]
- **Users' words:** The RSS feed is gone with the listing. Not verified.
- **Evidence:** https://www.mealime.com/closing (doc); AppGoblin https://appgoblin.info/apps/1079999103 (report: "removed from the US App Store"); `shots/mealime/strip.png` (seen).
- **What Otto can take:** (1) **Mealime's slot is open right now.** Its users are being sent into Albertsons store apps, and Otto has the same shape (quiz → week of dinners → aisle list). Target "Mealime alternative" in ASO and ads this quarter. (2) Copy the order: personal plan and list *before* the paywall, with the paywall soft and right after that moment.

## Ollie (ollie) · id6480014476 · ★4.77 (951 ratings, report) · subtitle "Healthy recipes & grocery list" (report; page now 404)
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** Getting rid of the nightly "what should we have for dinner?" argument with a spouse. Feeding picky kids.
- **Magic moment:** You tell the AI "my son doesn't like broccoli" and it swaps the side dish in tonight's recipe, then says "Memory updated" so it never suggests broccoli to him again.
- **Path to it:** Unknown screen count. A **payment method is required to start the 7-day trial** (doc text quoted by reports), so this is a hard paywall before value, or at least before the full plan. About $9.99/mo or roughly $7/mo yearly. [report: AOL/Inquirer, https://www.inquirer.com/food/ai-apps-meal-planning-shopping-20250828.html; the onboarding itself was not verified]
- **How it makes it personal:** It speaks in the first person ("Hi! I plan healthy family meals so you don't have to") and remembers named family members. The plan shows a family-sized "Your Menu" with Replace and Modify on every meal.
- **Store page pitch:** 1 = "Hi! I plan healthy family meals so you don't have to." + "Trusted by 75,000+ families" (a tilted phone showing "Your Menu", a Dinner card and an "Ask me anything" bar). 2 = "I magically plan meals just for you." (a recipe with High fiber/Gluten free chips and a "How was your dinner?" emoji rating). 3 = "I can adjust anything to make it yours." (a Customize recipe chat: the broccoli swap and a **"Memory updated"** tag). [seen]
- **Users' words:** The RSS feed is gone with the listing. Reports say it saves "up to five hours a week", that suggestions felt repetitive until users asked it to be "more inventive", and that old meals pile up in the list.
- **Evidence:** AppGoblin https://appgoblin.info/apps/6480014476 (report); `shots/ollie/strip.png` (seen); https://ollie.ai (doc; the company now focuses on SMS).
- **What Otto can take:** (1) "Ask Otto" should *visibly remember*. A small "Otto will remember: Leo doesn't eat broccoli" chip after a swap turns one chat into lasting personalization. (2) Ollie's screenshots use the mascot's first-person voice; Otto's otter can do the same ("I planned your week").

## Plan to Eat (plan-to-eat) · id1215348056 · ★4.84 (6,115 ratings) · subtitle "Meal Planner & Grocery Lists"
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** "I need a plan when life gets in the way": a family of picky or medical eaters, and one weekly shop.
- **Magic moment:** You drag your own clipped recipes onto days of the week and the shopping list writes itself, merged and grouped by store section.
- **Path to it:** Account sign-up first. "Try Plan to Eat FREE for 14 days, **no payment information required** at sign-up", and an annual subscription after that. The library starts empty, so value needs you to clip a few recipes (share sheet from Safari/Pinterest/IG) and plan a week. That's minutes to days, and many reviewers say it "clicked" after the first month. [doc: store description; steps inferred; not on Mobbin]
- **How it makes it personal:** It's 100% your own recipes, plus a "staples" list, leftovers and frozen-meal tracking. Nothing is generated.
- **Store page pitch:** 1 = "The Meal Planner trusted by 40,000+ families since 2009" (a month calendar with colour-coded meal rows). 2 = "Save recipes from anywhere" (a recipe page ringed by IG/FB/YouTube/TikTok logos, with Plan and Start Cooking buttons). 3 = "All your recipes in one place" (My Recipes grid). 4 = "An organized list made for you" (a shopping list with Kroger and On Hand filters and "Deliver Groceries"). [seen]
- **Users' words (reviews):** Long-time users (4–10 years) love that they drag a recipe onto a day and the list generates. They say it "keeps my life on track" (a dialysis patient planning for a family with gluten-free and heart diets) and some wait for the Black Friday 50% sale. Complaints: it's overpriced and imports are buggy.
- **Evidence:** iTunes lookup and RSS (doc); `shots/plan-to-eat/strip.png` (seen).
- **What Otto can take:** (1) A no-card trial lowers the bar to trying (ReciMe also shows "No Payment Now"). (2) An "On hand" filter on the list, where you tick what's already in the pantry before shopping, is a ritual long-time users love.

## Patterns across this group
- **Two moment types, and nobody joins them up.** *Capture* apps (ReciMe, Paprika, Plan to Eat, Samsung Food) deliver "my messy saves became a clean cookbook" right away. *Planner* apps (Mealime, Ollie, Samsung Food+) deliver "a week of dinners was made for me". The strongest first moment in each is concrete and immediate: ReciMe's caption-less video parse and Mealime's plan plus aisle list in about 90 s.
- **Time to value:** The winners show it within the first 1–2 minutes and *inside* onboarding. ReciMe runs a real import at screen 10 of 20, and Mealime's plan follows an 11-step quiz. Paprika and Plan to Eat hand you an empty box and depend on users building it up over weeks, which only works with a one-time price (Paprika) or a 16-year-old loyal base (Plan to Eat).
- **Paywall position:** The high-volume apps put the paywall *after* the first moment and make it soft. ReciMe: trial screen with an X and "No Payment Now", account skippable. Mealime: dismissible paywall straight after onboarding, free core. Samsung Food: free core with an AI upsell. Plan to Eat: 14-day trial with no card. Only Ollie asks for a card up front, and it is now gone from the US store. Otto is subscription-only with no free tier, so it **must** deliver the moment before the paywall. There is no free tier to recover the user afterwards.
- **Metering as a teaser:** ReciMe's visible "5/5" import counter (even on store screenshot 3, "3 left") turns the value into a currency you can count. It also produces the main 2-star complaint ("didn't say there was a limit").
- **How the personal reflection works:** Quiz answers alone don't feel personal. Samsung Food's ignored diet preferences become a 1-star review. What lands is *your own input visibly reused*: your saved reel parsed (ReciMe), your partner's list updating (Samsung Food), "Memory updated: no broccoli for your son" (Ollie).
- **Screenshot 1:** It's mostly a claim plus social proof, not UI. ReciMe has "World's most popular… 15 million+", Plan to Eat has "40,000+ families since 2009", Ollie has "75,000+ families", and Samsung Food has a celebrity. Only Paprika leads with a UI shot. The one that sells a *moment* is Ollie's first-person line "I plan healthy family meals so you don't have to", which names the job, not the feature.
- **Unclaimed or badly done: "from what I already saved to tonight's dinner, planned for my household."** ReciMe captures but barely plans (its "Magic Meal Plan" event suggests it knows this). Ollie and Mealime plan from *their* recipe catalogue, never yours. Plan to Eat makes you do the planning by hand. No one takes the user's imported reels, fits them to the household quiz (diet, time, size) and hands back a week with a shared, aisle-sorted list in one beat. "Your 5 saved TikToks just became Mon–Fri dinners for 4, list shared with Ana" is open, and Otto already has every piece of it (import, quiz, plan, shared list). Done badly across the board: **"what can I cook with what's in my fridge"**. Samsung Food hides it behind Food+ Vision AI, Ollie's fridge photo is a side feature, and there's no sign anyone does it well.
- **Market timing (doc):** Mealime, the free quiz → plan → list app, shut down on Oct 21, 2026, and Ollie's meal app is out of the US store. The "personal weekly plan in a minute" slot has fewer competitors right now than at any point in years.
