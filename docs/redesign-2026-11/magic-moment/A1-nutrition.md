## Cal AI (calai) · id6480417616 · ★4.80 (373,954 ratings) · subtitle "Food & Macro Counter"
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** counting calories took too many steps in other apps; reviewers want to "just take a picture" and move on.
- **Magic moment:** the first photo of a real plate comes back in seconds as a named dish, with calories, protein, carbs and fat tagged per item, and the "Calories left" ring on Home drops.
- **Path to it:** there are about 30 onboarding screens before Home (Figma 41:100, 34 screens). The order is questions → "Save your progress" (account, with **Skip**) → generating plan → plan ready → **hard paywall** → Home → + → camera → result. So the moment comes after the optional account and after the paywall. Scans need a subscription: the description says "food scanning analysis results require a subscription", and the FAQ says + opens the paywall when unsubscribed. Estimate: 3–5 min to the first scan [doc + seen(study) + inferred timing]. There is a *pre-paywall* moment: "Congratulations your custom plan is ready!" with a daily calorie number built from the user's own answers [seen(study)].
- **How it makes the moment personal:** the plan screen gives back the user's own goal weight and speed ("Gaining 6 kg is a realistic target", "Your daily recommendation… edit anytime"). The scan result names the user's own dish ("Pancakes with blueberries & syrup") and lets them "Fix Results".
- **Store page pitch:** screenshot 1 = "Make calorie tracking easy with AI". It shows Home with a big floating "1250 Calories left" card, macro rings and "Recently uploaded" photo meals. 2 = "Just snap a picture of your food" (camera framing a pancake stack, "Scan food" mode). 3 = "Then let us do the rest" (the same pancakes with floating labels Blueberries 8 / Pancakes 595 / Syrup 12, then 615 kcal and a health score of 7/10). 4 = "Track your progress as you go" (weight goal chart and a weekly macro bar chart). The screenshots tell one story in order: snap → result → progress [seen].
- **Users' words (reviews):** reviewers say it is easy: take a picture or scan and forget about it, with no need to pick breakfast or dinner. Several are losing weight after 3 months. Others say accuracy is around 80%, so it is not for strict deficits but fine for a "lazy" tracker. Negative reviews are about the trial billing and needing a card even to try it [doc: RSS mostHelpful].
- **Evidence:** iTunes lookup/RSS [doc]; shots/calai 1–4 [seen]; lanes/calai.json lanes 4, 5, 10, 14 [seen(study)]; 02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md l.30 [seen(study)]; SCREEN_REFERENCES.md l.37, 72, 392; founder interview: onboarding questions and animated screens each tested to *raise* conversion, attributed to users "invest[ing] more time"; sign-in moved to the end; 20–25% first-session trial/paid conversion (https://getlatka.com/interviews/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-2025, https://getlatka.com/blog/how-cal-ai-achieved-35-million-revenue-in-just-one-year) [report].
- **What Otto can take:** (1) Give screenshots 2→3 a single "input → result" story: a messy video or link goes in, a clean recipe or week comes out, each on its own screenshot. (2) Before the paywall, show one concrete number or result built from the user's answers. Cal AI's plan-ready screen is the moment the paywall leans on.

## YAZIO (yazio) · id946099227 · ★4.70 (51,643 ratings) · subtitle "Weight Loss Food Diary"
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** tracking calories without it feeling overwhelming or punishing. One reviewer loves that after logging, the app "tells you all the good things" about the food.
- **Magic moment:** the mascot greets the user by the name they picked ("Nice to meet you, Alex!"). Then the first Today screen shows *their* number (for example "1,476 Remaining"), and the first log, by barcode or AI photo, fills the ring and calls out positives like "Good source of protein".
- **Path to it:** the onboarding is about 18 screens (Mobbin flow 003557b1): goal → name → multi-goal → mascot reward "Way to shine, superstar! Only 57% get this far" → body data → "Sign in to start your journey with Yazio!" (Apple/Google/E-mail, **account required**) → Today. **No hard paywall**: there is a free tier, and Today carries a "Special Offer 75% OFF" card with a countdown. The first log is free (reviews say barcode scanning and macros work without Pro). Estimate: 2–3 min [seen(Mobbin) + doc(reviews) + inferred timing].
- **How it makes the moment personal:** the user's chosen name comes back in the mascot's mouth. Their calorie budget appears as "Remaining". Food feedback is framed positively.
- **Store page pitch:** screenshot 1 = "✓ Fasting ✓ Calories ✓ Weight loss". The Yettie mascot pops out of a salad bowl next to a dark Today screen (868 Remaining, AI-tagged meals). 2 is a panorama continuation of 1: a "4.6 App Store" laurel and an AI scan frame over eggs and avocado, then a "Breakfast 466/566 Cal · AI" chip. 3 = "Easy Food Scanning" (barcode on a protein bar, "Good source of protein" / "Very high in sugars" pills). 4 = "Intermittent fasting" (fasting timer 13:41:07, sleeping mascot) [seen].
- **Users' words (reviews):** "hooked by the second day", calling it organized and cute next to "plain" rivals. People are happy the free version stays usable. Complaints are about ads, interactive promos and gamified chests [doc].
- **Evidence:** lookup/RSS [doc]; shots/yazio 1–4 [seen]; Mobbin flows 003557b1 (Onboarding, 18 screens) and 7b27b6d5 (Creating account) [seen]; SCREEN_REFERENCES.md l.38, 202, 393; lanes/yazio.json lanes 4, 14 [seen(study)].
- **What Otto can take:** (1) Have the otter say the user's own name or household back at the end of the questionnaire, plus one reward screen. That is cheap, warm, and on-brand for a mascot app. (2) After each action, praise what is good about it ("this week covers 4 veggie dinners") instead of only showing numbers.

## MyFitnessPal (mfp) · id341232718 · ★4.71 (2,388,470 ratings) · subtitle "AI Coach & Nutrition Tracker"
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** "know what you're eating" and losing weight by logging consistently. The barcode scanner and the huge database are the reasons people give for staying for years.
- **Magic moment:** the first scan or search finds the exact branded food, and the user's personal daily calorie goal on Today ticks down. Reviewers describe it as "it helped me lose 56 lbs".
- **Path to it:** about 21–24 screens (Figma 37:221; Mobbin 6ec621e1): value carousel → **account choice up front** ("Let's customize" Continue/Google/Apple) → name, goals, habits, activity, body data, weekly goal → consents → **create account** → Premium 1-month trial paywall **with ×** (soft) → Today → log. So the moment comes after the account and after a skippable paywall. Estimate: 4–6 min [seen(study) + inferred timing].
- **How it makes the moment personal:** the user's goals and weekly target turn into a calorie budget. In the meal-planner path, MFP plays answers back explicitly: "We'll also take into account… Recipe variety 'Chinese and Mexican'", then "Hungry yet? Your meal plan is ready" right before the Premium+ price [seen(study) SCREEN_REFERENCES l.76].
- **Store page pitch:** screenshot 1 = "myfitnesspal · The #1 Food & Nutrition Tracking App". It is a salad photo with a scan bracket and "9 G CARBS" / "4 G PROTEIN" pills, with no phone frame. 2 = "Know What You're Eating" (verified-check item cards: Mixed salad greens 7 cal, Cherry tomatoes 14 cal, Edamame, Cucumber). 3 = "Make Every Meal Count." (weekly macro bar chart against goal %). 4 = "Scan Your Food" (meal scan of avocado toast listed as 3 items, "Log breakfast") [seen].
- **Users' words (reviews):** loyal for 4–8 years, it works with any plan, and the meal planner and grocery list for the family are new favourites. Complaints: ads, data loss, and paywalling things that used to be free [doc].
- **Evidence:** lookup/RSS [doc]; shots/mfp 1–4 [seen]; 02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md l.34; SCREEN_REFERENCES.md l.35, 76; lanes/mfp.json lanes 4, 6 [seen(study)].
- **What Otto can take:** (1) MFP's meal-planner reveal is the closest analogue to Otto. It reads back the user's specific answers ("Chinese and Mexican") on the reveal screen just before the price. Otto's "Otto's got your week" should name the user's inputs the same way. (2) Show real food with real numbers on screenshot 1, with no phone frame.

## Lifesum (lifesum) · id286906691 · ★4.65 (151,000 ratings) · subtitle "Food Diary & Macro Tracker App"
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** eating healthier without perfection. It is "the first app that I've actually lost weight with", and it helps build better habits.
- **Magic moment:** the "Your personalized plan is ready" reveal after "BUILD MY PLAN". Then, on the first log, the AI rates *this* meal against *your* goal ("quality lunch… supports your goal of gaining weight", food score 8).
- **Path to it:** about 17 screens (Figma 45:13). An account is offered on screen 3 but can be skipped. Then come the value carousel, questions with an interstitial ("Great—we've got your starting point…"), BUILD MY PLAN → "100% Finalizing your plan" → "Your personalized plan is ready / Create an account to begin" (**account right after the reveal**) → Premium paywall (50% off, Restore) → Diary with a "Try AI food tracking" card. The free version remains [seen(study) + doc(reviews)]. Estimate: 3–4 min [inferred].
- **How it makes the moment personal:** the paywall uses the user's name ("Alex, be 5x more likely…"), the chat greets "Good morning, Helen!", and the coach card on Home reads "Two weeks in and you're doing great! However, you ran a bit low on protein yesterday". Food ratings reference the user's goal.
- **Store page pitch:** screenshot 1 = "Healthy eating. Simplified by AI." It shows the Diary with 856 kcal remaining, a "Good morning! Two weeks in and you're doing great…" coach note and tracked photo foods. 2 = "Just snap it" (camera on a tofu bowl, then a card "Tofu with quinoa tabbouleh… 528 kcal"). 3 = "Track with ease" (chat-style "What do you want to track?" with "Often tracked for breakfast" chips, camera and mic). 4 = "Get insights" (food rating 8 with "Good source of fibre" and a goal-referenced explanation) [seen].
- **Users' words (reviews):** reviewers mention the beautiful design again and again, along with the dos and don'ts guidance. Meal plans and the shopping list disappoint: items have to be added one by one, and duplicates aren't merged. There are complaints about the new AI update being glitchy and annual billing surprises [doc].
- **Evidence:** lookup/RSS [doc]; shots/lifesum 1–4 [seen]; SCREEN_REFERENCES.md l.41, 74, 79, 394, 433; lanes/lifesum.json lanes 4, 14 [seen(study)].
- **What Otto can take:** (1) On screenshot 1, put a coach-voice message that shows the app *knows you* ("Two weeks in… low on protein"). For Otto: an otter note like "Three dinners left this week, Tuesday's is 20 min". (2) Lifesum users complain that the shopping list isn't auto-built or merged. Otto's merged, aisle-grouped, shared list is a proven pain point worth showing as the outcome of a plan.

## MacroFactor (macrofactor) · id1553503471 · ★4.84 (23,788 ratings) · subtitle "Calorie Counter & Food Log"
- **Problem it solves (user's words):** plateaus and guesswork. Users want a tool that "can use my data to create a plan… and adjust with me". It is "the only nutrition app that's worked for me".
- **Magic moment:** the onboarding estimate is shown as a chat ("2720 kcal" with replies "How did you calculate that?"). The deeper moment is delayed: after the first week of logging, the expenditure estimate starts moving and the first weekly check-in adjusts targets to *your* data. That moment comes days in, not minutes.
- **Path to it:** **trial-first paywall** ("Try 1 Week for Free", plan picker, StoreKit) → "You're in. Create your account to continue." (**account after paying**) → a chaptered "Let's get started" checklist (Basics → Goal → Program, ticked between chapters, about 20 screens) → calorie estimate → dashboard. **There is no free tier.** So it is paywall → account → questions → estimate, and the real aha comes after about 7 days [seen(study) lanes 4, 5, 14; SCREEN_REFERENCES l.39, 390; report: screensdesign.com/showcase/macrofactor-macro-tracker says the trial is offered before the detailed questions; doc: knowledge base via macrofactor.com/expenditure-v3/, estimate "starts moving" after about a week].
- **How it makes the moment personal:** it explains why each question matters ("Your weight history can impact your current metabolism"). The estimate can be questioned in chat, and every check-in shows numbers computed from the user's own logs and weigh-ins.
- **Store page pitch:** screenshot 1 = "MACROFACTOR · SMART MACRO TRACKER". It is a pure brand image (heirloom tomatoes on grey), with no UI and no value shown. 2 = "LOG FOODS FASTER" (logger with "3 PM Picks" time-based suggestions and Scan/Search/AI/Quick Add). 3 = "MASTER YOUR METABOLISM" (expenditure chart: average 2940 kcal, +268 change, flux range). 4 = "SNAP FOODS WITH AI" (photo logger on a yogurt dessert) [seen].
- **Users' words (reviews):** "I actually enjoy logging", "like going from email to text". It is backed by science and "doesn't require hitting macros exactly". Critics say the algorithm cut calories too aggressively in the early weeks [doc].
- **Evidence:** lookup/RSS [doc]; shots/macrofactor 1–4 [seen]; lanes/macrofactor.json lanes 4, 5, 14 [seen(study)]; https://macrofactor.com/expenditure-v3/ [doc]; https://screensdesign.com/showcase/macrofactor-macro-tracker [report].
- **What Otto can take:** (1) Like Otto, it is subscription-only, and it gets away with the paywall up front because it sells trust in a *method*: it explains its reasoning and lets users ask "how did you calculate that?". Otto's plan reveal could carry a "why these dinners?" reply. (2) Add a second, delayed magic moment: a week-2 check-in where Otto adapts the plan to what the household actually cooked or skipped.

## Patterns across this group
- **Input → instant, specific output is the core magic.** Four of the five (all but MacroFactor) build the moment around "snap/scan → named food + numbers in seconds". The result names the user's own dish and lets them fix it. The magic is a transformation of *their* thing, not browsing content.
- **The personal number comes first, before any logging.** Every app turns the questionnaire into one personal figure: daily calories or a plan. They reveal it with ceremony ("Congratulations your custom plan is ready", "100% Finalizing your plan", "Hungry yet? Your meal plan is ready"). That reveal is the pre-paywall magic moment. The scan or log is the post-paywall one.
- **Reflecting the user back works and costs little.** Examples: the mascot says your name (YAZIO), the paywall uses it (Lifesum), the plan is read back ("Chinese and Mexican", MFP), and a coach note uses your data (Lifesum, "low on protein yesterday"). Cal AI's founder reports that *more* questions raised conversion because users invest time [report]. That only holds when the answers visibly come back.
- **Time-to-value is 2–6 minutes of questions.** None of them gets to the first log in under about 2 min. Long questionnaires (18–34 screens) are normal here because each one ends in a personal result. MacroFactor's deepest aha takes about 7 days (adaptive check-in).
- **Where the paywall sits:** right after the plan reveal and before the first real use for Cal AI (hard) and MFP/Lifesum (soft, with × or a free tier). YAZIO puts no wall in the flow, just an offer card on Today. MacroFactor puts it *before* the questions, and that only works with a strong method-trust brand. The account generally comes *after* the reveal, framed as "save your progress" (Cal AI, Lifesum). The exceptions are MFP and YAZIO (before Today) and MacroFactor (after payment).
- **Screenshot 1 sells the outcome on a home screen or real food, not features.** Cal AI, YAZIO and Lifesum show the dashboard with a big "calories left/remaining" number. MFP shows real food with nutrient pills. MacroFactor's brand-only image is the outlier. The strongest sequences (Cal AI, Lifesum) then run 2 = input, 3 = result, 4 = progress.
- **The mascot appears only in YAZIO**, and there it carries the warmth: a reward screen, greeting by name, and the store image. That is directly relevant to Otto's otter. YAZIO also shows that a mascot can sit alongside heavy upsell, which reviewers resent.
