## Duolingo (duolingo) · id570060128 · ★4.72 (5,556,431 ratings) · subtitle "Spanish, French, Chess & Math"
- Problem it solves (user's words, one line): "I want to learn a language but it feels like work." The reviews' version: it "feels like a game" and "makes me feel like I have made a lot of progress."
- Magic moment (one sentence: the instant, concrete): You finish your first bite-sized lesson, the results screen shows XP, accuracy and time, and you realise you already understood real sentences in a new language a few minutes after opening the app.
- Path to it: about 20 screens (7 quick questions with Duo, a notification primer, from-scratch vs. placement), then the first lesson. This is **before account** (the profile wall comes after the placement/lesson and has "Later") and **before any paywall** (no paywall in onboarding; Super is sold later, when hearts or ads start to bite). Roughly 3–5 minutes [seen: Figma 46:116 + Mobbin flows in 02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md; time inferred].
- How it makes the moment personal: the questions about your language, why you're learning, level and daily goal come back to you as "50 words in week 1", "what you can achieve in 3 months" and a placement result ("Start at Score 10"). The lesson is pitched at the level you chose [seen].
- Store page pitch: screenshot 1 = "learn 40+ languages for free" (shows the Courses list: Spanish, French, German … Chess, Math, Music); 2 = "fun, bite-sized lessons" (a "Select the correct image" exercise, 牛乳 → milk); 3 = "solve chess puzzles" (a checkmate puzzle with a character); 4 = "build language skills" (translating "¡Necesito más café!" → "I need more coffee!") [seen]. All four show the lesson itself rather than outcomes.
- Users' words (reviews, paraphrased): reviewers say it feels like a game and that it "congratulates you for getting a question right". Not breaking the streak is the motivation they keep coming back for. One says they understand French better and "feel smarter", and several like that it's free and you can still do many lessons a day without paying [doc: RSS].
- Evidence: iTunes lookup/RSS; shots/duolingo; lanes/duolingo.json (lanes 1, 4, 14); research/02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md; maps/duolingo.html. Report: Appcues credits delaying sign-up with a 20% DAU lift (no primary source, treat as unverified): https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/duolingo-user-onboarding · https://www.appcues.com/blog/the-10-best-user-onboarding-experiences
- What Otto can take: (1) Let the first real use happen before the account wall and the paywall: one finished thing (a real lesson there; for Otto, a real dinner picked or cooked), then "save your progress". (2) Turn the questionnaire answers into a concrete promise with a number ("50 words in week 1", which for Otto could be "5 dinners sorted this week, ~2 h saved").

## Headspace (headspace) · id493145008 · ★4.84 (973,769 ratings) · subtitle "Mental Health, Stress, Anxiety"
- Problem it solves: "My anxiety and stress are getting bad, and I could never stick with meditation."
- Magic moment: The first guided session ends (a few minutes of breathing with Andy or Eve in Basics) and you notice you actually feel calmer. That relief is the moment.
- Path to it: "Breathe in" splash → **create account (~screen 2)** → what's on your mind → quiz intro ("we'll reveal your course") → style quiz ×5 → **paywall with a trial timeline** → result "For Calm Explorers like you" → Today → first session. That is about 10+ screens, **after account** and **after paywall** (soft, with a trial). The recommended course is revealed only after the paywall. Roughly 3–5 minutes to the first session [seen: Mobbin 31b21791 via 02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md; time inferred].
- How it makes the moment personal: the quiz is framed as "we'll reveal your course" and pays off with "For Calm Explorers like you" and a matched course. The Today tab greets you by name ("Good Evening, Alex / Start your day") [seen].
- Store page pitch: screenshot 1 = "Award Winning" (headspace logo, smiling orange sun, Apple Design Award + Webby laurels; no product UI); 2 = "Stress less" (Explore grid: Meditate/Sleep/Move/Music, a "Managing Stress" guided program card); 3 = "Sleep soundly" (Sleep screen: Sleepcasts, Wind Downs, Sleep Music); 4 = "Learn to meditate in a few minutes a day" (Basics course, "Choose your teacher: Andy / Eve", Begin Course) [seen]. Screenshot 1 sells trust, not the moment.
- Users' words: people turn to it when stress, health anxiety or pain flares and it helps them calm down "in the moment". One reviewer tried the 14-day trial, noticed over time that things stopped stressing them as much, and bought it. Others had never stuck with meditation and now do, "even 5 minutes a day" [doc: RSS].
- Evidence: iTunes lookup/RSS; shots/headspace; lanes/headspace.json (1, 3, 4, 14); 02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md; maps/headspace.html.
- What Otto can take: (1) Use quiz-as-reveal: frame Otto's 7 questions as "we'll build your week" and end on "For [household] like yours", so the questions feel like they produce something. (2) A cautionary point: Headspace hides the result behind the paywall and its moment is a feeling that needs a session. Otto can show its result (the week) before asking for money.

## Finch: Self-Care Pet (finch) · id1528595748 · ★4.95 (771,583 ratings) · subtitle "Daily Journal & Habit Tracker"
- Problem it solves: "Some days I can't even get out of bed or brush my teeth; self-care feels like a chore."
- Magic moment: You check off a tiny goal like "Get out of bed", your newly hatched birb gains energy for its first adventure, and you feel rewarded for something that felt impossible a minute ago.
- Path to it: 21-screen onboarding. Splash → egg/pet setup → "You hatched a birb!" (~screen 6) → naming and questions → "Get reminders from Lee" primer (Maybe later) → "DAY 1 … Help Baby Lee gain full energy today" (Start today) → Home with default goals ("7 goals left for today!", "1st Adventure 0/15"). **No account needed** (local-only by default). The core loop is **free forever**; a Plus trial offer is in the flow, but its exact position relative to Home was not confirmed. Roughly 2–4 minutes [seen: Mobbin flow 80ef83ef, 5 sampled screens; lanes/finch.json lanes 4, 12, 14; time inferred].
- How it makes the moment personal: you name the pet and it carries that name everywhere ("Baby Lee has butterflies seeing you", reminders "From Lee"). Goals come from your answers, and you can also write your own [seen; personalisation of goals per description, doc].
- Store page pitch: screenshot 1 = "Self-care is better together" (two birbs hugging, trophy and heart bubbles; no UI); 2 = "Build habits with friends by your side" (Goal Buddies: Drink water / Stretch / Eat veggies, checked by two birbs); 3 = "Send each other support and silliness" (a Valentine to "Bagel & Sam"); 4 = "Grow, explore, and adventure together" (Adventuring timer, "3 goals left today!", Get out of bed / Brush teeth / Wash my face) [seen]. The current store pitch leads with friends; the solo moment only shows up in screenshot 4.
- Users' words: a bedridden user says "something about taking care of this bird" got them moving, and simple goals with a reward helped "so much". Another titled their review "Finally something worked" after years of ADHD struggles. Users describe earning rewards for small acts like folding laundry [doc: RSS].
- Evidence: iTunes lookup/RSS; shots/finch; Mobbin https://mobbin.com/flows/80ef83ef-f872-4825-b18d-6b193d60a9aa; lanes/finch.json. Report: founders cite Jane McGonigal and gamifying life (https://dishabhatia.substack.com/p/the-self-care-bird-app-finch); no activation metric was found.
- What Otto can take: (1) Have the mascot react to the user's first real action. When the first dinner is planned or cooked, the otter visibly responds (a Day 1 card, "Otto's happy you're cooking tonight"). (2) Seed the first screen with tiny pre-filled wins, the way Finch's default goals do, so Home is never empty. For Otto, tonight's dinner should already be chosen and one tap from "Cook".

## Flo Period Tracker (flo) · id1038369065 · ★4.74 (2,013,524 ratings) · subtitle "Ovulation & Pregnancy Calendar"
- Problem it solves: "I never know when my period is coming, or whether what I'm feeling is normal."
- Magic moment: Right after you enter your last period and cycle details, Today shows "Period in: N days" with your fertile window circled on the week strip. Your own body has turned into a forecast.
- Path to it: consents (health processing, privacy policy, tracking) → "for yourself?" → goals → reasons → … → **semi-hard "Choose your plan" paywall** (Go back / limited-access link; dismissing it brings up "46% OFF FOREVER") → account ask → "Setting up" spinners → Today ("Period in 4 days"). That is about 15–20 screens, with the prediction landing **after the paywall** (it is shown, and dismissible) and after an account ask. The cycle-date questions were not individually observed. Roughly 3–5 minutes [seen: Mobbin 3e01b864 and lanes/flo.json lanes 1, 4, 11, 14; screen count and time inferred].
- How it makes the moment personal: the prediction is literally your data reflected back (days until your period, today's chance of pregnancy). Later insights match symptoms you logged ("My daily insights", Symptom Checker "PCOS: significant match") [seen in screenshots].
- Store page pitch: screenshot 1 = "Predict your next period" (Today: week strip with circled days, "Period in: 2 days", "Chances of getting pregnant are low", Log period, My daily insights); 2 = "Maximize your connection" (Flo for Partners, push "James, it's show time … Ovulation is today!"); 3 = "Got symptoms? Get answers" (Symptom Checker: PCOS / Endometriosis / Perimenopause matches); 4 = "Monitor your fertile days" (calendar, "Prediction: Day of ovulation", chance "High") [seen]. Screenshot 1 shows the moment exactly.
- Users' words: users who tracked for years say the patterns built up into something real. One got a "your symptoms match … PCOS" nudge that later turned out to be right. Another discovered a 45+ day cycle and went to their OB-GYN. A 28-year-old says they "can't believe" how much they didn't know about their own body [doc: RSS].
- Evidence: iTunes lookup/RSS; shots/flo; lanes/flo.json; 02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md (Flo row).
- What Otto can take: (1) Make screenshot 1 and the first post-onboarding screen the same personal forecast. For Otto: "Your week: 5 dinners, 32 min avg, shopping list ready", built from the questionnaire. (2) Promise that the forecast gets better with use: Flo's predictions sharpen as you log, and Otto's plan could get visibly smarter after each cooked or skipped meal.

- Problem it solves: "Budgeting apps overwhelm me; I just want to actually keep track of my spending."

- Path to it: unknown. The app is not on Mobbin, and no onboarding was observed. There is a 14-day trial ("no credit card needed" per the site), so the first use is probably **before the paywall**. - How it makes the moment personal: your own categories and budgets ("Coffee shops $60 / $60 left", "Dining Out $300 / $287") and a lock-screen widget showing "This week $1,142 spent, 2 days left" [seen in screenshots].
- Store page pitch: screenshot 1 = "A better way to budget" (budget home Jul 28–Aug 3: weekly categories with budgeted/left in green, orange and red pills); 2 = "Super simple expense tracking" (Transactions list); 3 = "Home & lockscreen widgets and tap to quick capture transactions" ("$1,135 of $1,405 spent this week" widget, lock screen); 4 = "Reminders to help you build a habit" (push "It's going to be a good day! ## Tiimo visual planner (tiimo) · id1480220328 · ★4.60 (21,370 ratings) · subtitle "Schedule Structured Daily Task"
- Problem it solves: "My ADHD makes time management a struggle; paper planners only work for a little while."
- Magic moment: You type a messy brain dump ("call my mom, eat breakfast, bike to work, pick up my kids") and the AI co-planner turns it into a visual timeline of your actual day, with times, icons and colours, ready to accept with "Create tasks".
- Path to it: splash → **sign up (Apple/email) at screen 2** → email form ×3 → marketing opt-in ×2 → biggest need ×2 → neurodivergent? ×2 → "500,000 happy users" → **paywall (annual 7-day trial, ×)** → notifications (Skip) → import calendar → Today. That is about 16 screens, **after account** and **after paywall**, before any value is shown. The AI brain dump is reachable from Today ("What's the plan today?"). Roughly 2–4 minutes to Today [seen: Figma 42:8 via 02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md; lanes/tiimo.json lanes 10, 14; time inferred].
- How it makes the moment personal: your own words come back as your own schedule. The chat reply "Alright, here you go" sits above tasks built from your sentence [seen in screenshot 2], and it proposes tasks for review rather than forcing them [seen: lane 10].
- Store page pitch: screenshot 1 = "Your to-do list planner" (Today with Anytime/Morning/Day/Evening, plus App of the Year 2025, ADA finalist and Editors' Choice laurels); 2 = a five-star quote, "This app has literally changed my life." — Laura, "Over 45.000 5-star reviews", above the brain-dump → timeline chat; 3 = "Organize daily tasks" (Tuesday timeline); 4 = "Schedule reminders: Never forget anything" (lock-screen Focus Live Activity) [seen].
- Users' words: users who are newly aware of their ADHD say they'd looked "for a long time" and it explained their struggle. Another says their days go differently after 2 months and their self-esteem is up. A telling complaint: removing the completion confetti made the app feel "utilitarian", because the celebration was part of the value [doc: RSS].
- Evidence: iTunes lookup/RSS; shots/tiimo; lanes/tiimo.json; 02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md; maps/tiimo.html.
- What Otto can take: (1) A messy-input-to-tidy-plan moment for "Ask Otto": the user says "chicken, half a bag of spinach, 30 minutes, kid hates onions" and gets tonight's dinner, or a whole week, laid out visually and ready to accept. (2) Keep a small celebration when a meal is cooked or the shopping is done. Tiimo's reviews show that removing it costs goodwill.

## Waterllama (waterllama) · id1454778585 · ★4.87 (164,148 ratings) · subtitle "Hydration drink reminder - app"
- Problem it solves: "I never paid attention to how much water I drink, even though my doctor kept bringing it up."
- Magic moment: You tap your first glass and the empty llama silhouette starts filling with colour from the feet up, "330ml · 16% of your goal". Logging a drink is fun the very first time.
- Path to it: splash → "Track your intake" (Get started) → "Add your first sip" over a grey llama, with a units sheet → first sip logged → setup questions continue *on top of* the filling llama ("What's the first day of your week?") → day view. The first sip comes at about **screen 3–4 of 20, with no account at all**, and before the Health and notification primers and any paywall (Premium is sold later). About 30 seconds [seen: Mobbin flow 614a9757, 5 sampled screens; lanes/waterllama.json lanes 3, 12, 14; time inferred].
- How it makes the moment personal: the goal is calculated from your weight, activity and weather, and the llama fills as a percentage of *your* goal. Each drink type you pick (water, green tea, coffee) shows in its own colour [doc: description; seen].
- Store page pitch: screenshot 1 = "Water tracker" (200,000+ ratings, 4 Million users, Vogue/Yahoo/ABC/Healthline, Apple Design Award finalist 2022; no UI); 2 = "Drink water reminders" (a llama sipping on a pool float); 3 = "Track water from anything you drink" (striped llama at "52oz, 80% of your goal", drink row, Watch); 4 = "Interactive Widgets that motivate you to reach your goal" (widgets at 28oz/34oz/64oz with a 100% confetti state) [seen].
- Users' words: a day-one reviewer says it's "fun to tap" and watch the llama fill, and didn't expect it to also show how much is left for the day. Others say it has already changed their habits and made the goals feel achievable. The critical review asks why pay when premium is mostly cute extras [doc: RSS].
- Evidence: iTunes lookup/RSS; shots/waterllama; Mobbin https://mobbin.com/flows/614a9757-c571-4c51-9ded-8042ff223a07; lanes/waterllama.json.
- What Otto can take: (1) Ask for the first real action before the rest of setup. Waterllama's "Add your first sip" comes before the settings questions; for Otto it could be "Pick tonight's dinner", or "paste one recipe link" that imports live, before questions 2–7. (2) A visible fill metaphor for the week: the otter's plate or pantry fills as dinners are planned and cooked ("3 of 5 dinners sorted").

## Patterns across this group
- **The strongest moments are one physical tap with an instant, visible change.** The llama fills, the birb gains energy, the lesson awards XP, the brain dump becomes a timeline. They're felt within seconds of the first real action, not explained. Headspace is the exception, since its moment (calm) needs a full session, and it leans hardest on a paywall and trial to bridge that gap.
- **Time to value splits two ways:** under a minute and before setup for Waterllama (first sip at ~screen 3), a few minutes and before the account for Duolingo and Finch. Tiimo, Headspace and Flo put sign-up and/or the paywall *before* the moment, and they compensate with quizzes, social proof and trial timelines.
- **Personal reflection is the hook in every case:** your pet's name in its reminders ("From Lee"), your goal as a %, your days until your period, your sentence turned into your day, your placement level. The questionnaire pays off only when its answers come back as one concrete artefact ("For Calm Explorers like you", "50 words in week 1").
- **The paywall sits relative to the moment in three ways.** It can come after the moment and the habit (Duolingo, Finch free core, Waterllama premium extras), right before the result (Headspace reveals the course after the paywall, Flo shows the forecast after a semi-hard plan screen), or before any value (Tiimo). The free-first apps have the most and highest ratings in this group (Finch ★4.95/771k, Waterllama ★4.87/164k, Duolingo 5.5M).
- **A mascot reacting to the user is the emotional multiplier:** Duo, the birb and the llama all respond to the user's action. Reviews speak of caring for the bird and of the llama being "so stinking cute". Tiimo's confetti complaint shows the celebration itself is felt as value.
- **Screenshot 1 sells the moment in two of seven** (Flo's "Period in 2 days", and Luna's budget home with "left" amounts). Headspace, Waterllama and Tiimo lead with awards and social proof; Duolingo leads with breadth; Finch with friends. Where screenshot 1 shows the user's personalised result, the caption names the outcome ("Predict your next period").
- **The moment comes back every day through widgets, lock screen and Live Activities** (Luna, Waterllama, Tiimo, Duolingo). The best ones reduce it to one number you can glance at, which keeps the moment alive after day one.
