Taking Otto to the next level

Every screen we have, what is wrong with it, how better apps handle it, and the order to rebuild it. Nothing here has been changed in the app yet.

The short version

  • Rebuild the experience, keep the engine. Recipes, import, nutrition, payments and accounts work. What needs starting over is the look, the navigation, the plan and the first-run flow.
  • The "AI slop" feeling has a real cause. Warm cream, a serif headline and a terracotta accent is one of the most common looks AI tools produce. Otto uses exactly that, on nearly every screen.
  • Your otter is the asset nobody can copy. Today it is mostly a still image. It should become the thing that makes the app feel alive.
  • Fix the three reported bugs first as a quick 1.0.23, then start the redesign.

The bugs you reported

I traced these by reading the code. I have not reproduced them on a device, so each fix gets tested with your paid account.

Paid members see the paywall after signing in, with an X

Likely cause: these are one bug. Right after sign-in, the app asks about your membership before RevenueCat, the payment service, has switched to your account. It gets "not a member" and opens the paywall. Moments later the answer corrects itself, but nothing moves you off that screen.

The X only ever appears for members, which fits your report: you were seeing the members' version of the screen by accident.

Fix: wait for the account switch to finish before choosing a screen, and send members to the app automatically. Non-members already have no X. From Account, the membership page gets a normal back arrow.

Small otter on sign-in and sign-up

Cause: the screen draws a tall framed painting, background included, at only 84 points. It reads as a tiny pasted picture. Fix: replace it in the redesign with a large cut-out or animated otter.

Wrong image size on the paywall

Cause: the image component forces every picture into a square. The floating otter is wide, so it shrinks and leaves empty space above and below. Fix: let the image keep its real shape. This also corrects other wide art in the app.

Onboarding only once

Already correct in the code. It is saved on the account, so it also survives a reinstall or a new phone.

Why it looks dead today

  • Everything has the same weight. Plan, chat and Account are all the same white rounded cards on cream, so nothing leads the eye.
  • Food is too small. A recipe app should sell appetite. The plan shows a thumbnail surrounded by empty cards reading "Nothing planned".
  • Icon puzzles. Each planned meal has four unlabeled icons in a row: swap, cooked, leftovers and remove.
  • The app has no daily reason to open. Discover greets you with a catalog, but it does not know what you planned for tonight.
  • It stays inside the app. I found no home screen widget or lock screen timer, even though cooking suits both well.
  • It does not look like current iOS. The tab bar and sheets are custom drawn, so they miss the newest system look and behavior. (Dark mode was later ruled out: light only, ADR-0007.)

Meal plan: rebuilt around dates

You asked for dates, and that is the right spine. One finding from the research, though: Crouton also stacks day cards, much like ours. What separates better planners is photos, meal sections and a fast way to move between days.

  • Week strip on top. Tap a date or swipe between weeks. Dots show which days have food. Tiimo uses this pattern.
  • Tonight is the hero. A large photo with one action: Start cooking.
  • Breakfast, lunch and dinner sections, as Crouton does, each optional. People who only plan dinner never see empty guilt rows.
  • Press and drag to move a meal to another day. Swipe to mark it cooked or remove it. A long press opens swap and leftovers, with words instead of mystery icons.
  • "Plan my week" with Otto. Otto proposes meals, and you keep or swap each one with a tap, then get one grocery list.
  • Month view to look back and repeat a good week.

A new app structure

Today the tabs are Discover, Cookbook, a plus button, Plan and Account. I propose this instead:

  • Today (new home). Tonight's dinner, a timer you left running, what to defrost, and one idea from Otto.
  • Plan. The dated week above, with Shopping one tap inside it.
  • Plus button. One menu for Ask Otto, paste a link, scan a photo, or write a recipe.
  • Cookbook. Your saved recipes, as folders with cover photos.
  • Discover. Curated collections and search.
  • Account moves to your avatar at the top of Today, which frees a tab.

What makes it feel alive

  • A living otter. Interactive animation that reacts: stirs while AI works, cheers when you finish, naps on empty pages. Tools like Rive support this. It needs new animation art, and it will be the largest creative cost.
  • A new visual identity. A food-first layout and a type and color system that leaves the cream, serif and terracotta formula behind. I would show you 2 or 3 directions in Figma before any code.
  • Native iOS parts. System tab bar, sheets and search, so Otto gets the current iOS look and feel for free. A short technical test comes first, because some of this is still maturing in Expo, our app framework.
  • Motion with a purpose. Photos that carry from card to full screen, meals that drag between days, and haptics when you check something off.

Rules that stay, even in a fresh start

I set aside our old internal design rules for this plan. Apple's rules are different: you asked to follow them, and breaking them gets the app rejected or removed.

  • Paywall: the billed price stays the most prominent price. Trial length, restore, terms and privacy must be present. This is what got 1.0.21 rejected.
  • No X for non-members is allowed, but sign out and delete account must stay reachable, as they are now.
  • The AI provider must be named correctly. The app and privacy policy still say Anthropic while data goes to OpenAI. The app working does not resolve this. 1.0.22 is already in review with that text, so I want to correct it in 1.0.23 and ask users to consent again.
  • Nutrition stays an estimate, with its source shown, and reviews use only Apple's own prompt.

Decisions for you

  1. Start Phase 0 now? I recommend yes, because members hitting the paywall hurts your new testers today.
  2. Should the hard paywall stay? I recommend keeping it, but showing the user's generated first week before it. Letting people in for free would change the business model.
  3. Should the look change completely, keeping only the otter? I recommend yes. Otherwise it keeps reading as the same app.
  4. Do you agree with Today as the new home tab? It is the biggest structural change here.
  5. Who makes the otter animation? It can be you, a hired animator, or me working from your art with simpler motion. The quality gap between these is large.
