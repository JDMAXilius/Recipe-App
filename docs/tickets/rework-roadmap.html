2. The shape of the change

The new flow moves value before the account. That single change drives most of the code work:

today:   splash → sign up → 3 paintings → paywall → 5 tabs
next:    launch shell → Welcome → 3 questions → your first week → save your plan (account)
         → Otto Club (no X) → App Store sheet → you're all set → reminders → 4 tabs + Otto

What that means in code, in one breath:

  1. A draft lives on the phone before there is an account. The answers and the generated week are held locally, then claimed into the account the moment sign-up finishes.
  2. The gate learns more states. Welcome (not sign-up) is the signed-out door; "has a draft" sends a returning signed-out person back to Save your plan; lapsed and pending get their own screens.
  3. The paywall turns outcomes into screens, and reminders move to after the purchase.
  4. The tab bar is rebuilt: Home (Tonight + This week + Discover), Plan (with the list), Cookbook, Account, and Otto, the AI, as the separate circle at the trailing end (the spot iOS 26 gives Search; Juan, 2026-11-04: talking with Otto is the app's big feature). The ＋ Create tab goes away. Search becomes a screen pushed from Discover's search field.
  5. Doors get wired: universal links, notification taps, What's new, offline, not found — all through one "where was this person going" intent that survives sign-in and purchase.

4. Phases

Each phase is a set of small PRs that keep main shippable. Phases 0–3 are the flow; 4–8 the tabs and features; 9 is the Apple polish pass; 10 is release. Sizes are rough: S ≤ ½ day, M ≈ 1–2 days, L ≈ 3–5 days.
