# Plan how Otto AI acts for you
Status: draft · planning only · nothing here is built, scheduled or ticketed · Last verified against code at commit d03c6fc1 (no `src/`, `supabase/`, `app/` changes up to b3c532ed) · 2026-11-04 · Research synthesis and proposed design. Every OpenAI price and model name below comes from third-party trackers because OpenAI's own pages could not be reached; costs are **estimates** until checked there.

The proposal: Otto should take AI to the next level by turning Otto AI from a recipe composer into **an agent that changes the user's own plan, list, cookbook and reminders, through one typed action catalogue that the UI, the AI, Siri and widgets all call**. This is the evidence's clearest pattern. The food products that win commercially let AI *act on the object*: Instacart in ChatGPT and Tesco go from "what's for dinner?" to a filled basket, and Cal AI turned "photo → log" into a business MyFitnessPal bought. Pure recipe chat (Ask Instacart 2023, Yummly) never became a durable product on its own ([Instacart](https://investors.instacart.com/news-releases/news-release-details/instacart-app-launches-openai-chatgpt-first-company-offer-new); [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/tesco-launches-meal-planning-basket-building-in-app-ai-assistant/717474.article); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/cal-ai-sells-to-myfitnesspal-after-rapid-growth-f789c949); [The Spoon](https://thespoon.tech/whirlpool-lays-off-entire-team-for-cooking-and-recipe-app-yummly)). Nothing in this research argues for replacing D8. "Tonight is answered" stays the first magic moment: it comes before the account, uses no AI and costs nothing. AI adds a **second magic moment once subscribed, "Ask Otto and it's done"**. The user says or types one sentence, the week changes in place, a card shows what changed, and an Undo sits under it. A **third moment, the photo**, adds a plated dish that becomes a credited recipe with *~* nutrition. The proposed architecture is one `otto-agent` Edge Function. It runs a capped OpenAI Responses tool loop on the server with the user's own JWT, so RLS governs AI actions exactly as it governs taps. Its 17 tools are tiered by risk into act-then-undo, preview-then-tap and confirm. Allergy filtering lives in code, the model only *identifies* food and USDA computes the numbers, and voice stays on Apple's speech engine on the device. The proposed order, for whenever building starts: the action layer and its prerequisites in Phase 0, the agent inside the R3.4 Otto tab, then pressable cards in R4.2, then the photo moment in Phase 6. Native surfaces (App Intents, widget, Live Activity) come last, because Otto has none of the scaffolding today and the Expo tooling for them is alpha. Text agent turns cost an estimated **$0.10–$1.00 per active user a month**. Only realtime voice would cost real money, and that is why it is rejected for v1.

## The second magic moment is an action, not a conversation

D8's logic still holds, and these notes strengthen it. Otto's hard paywall (ADR-0005) puts every route behind an account and an active Club, and ADR-0006 requires named OpenAI consent before the first AI call. A pre-paywall AI moment would therefore need consent, an account and model spend on people who have not paid, all before the reveal. The reveal from `otto_recipes` needs none of these ([REWORK_ROADMAP D8, R1.4](../../tickets/REWORK_ROADMAP.md)). The photo moment is the tempting alternative first impression, and it is the wrong one. In a 2026 NIH test of 102 weighed meals, all four photo-calorie apps undercounted by **252–345 kcal per meal, about a third of the calories**, and fat was the biggest miss ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20260804/ai-photobased-calorietracking-tools-underestimate-them-by-33); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260726015237.htm)). Accuracy complaints are also the top Cal AI review theme ([Kimola](https://kimola.com/reports/unveil-user-insights-on-cal-ai-calorie-tracker-app-store-us-155190)). A first impression that is a third wrong does not fit Otto's honesty laws.

The evidence therefore extends D8 rather than overturning it. The chain becomes **reveal (no AI) → paywall → "Ask Otto and it's done" in the trial's first days → photo as a delight moment**. Each step should move the North Star, planned dinners cooked. The second moment is specific: the user types or says "swap Thursday, I've got chicken thighs", the Thursday card on screen changes to a chicken-thigh dinner that obeys the household's allergies, and a receipt line reads "Moved salmon off Thursday · Undo". Its activation proxy is **the first Otto action applied in the trial and not undone within ten minutes**. That proxy is measurable because every agent action is logged (below), and it is honest because an undone action counts as a miss. D8's paywall variant "Keep your week" (R2.1) can carry the promise as copy, for example "Then just tell Otto what to change". That is a sentence, not a button, so the no-dead-buttons rule holds. One more lesson argues against a separate AI tier. Duolingo Max reached only **5–8% of subscribers**, and its best AI explainer later became free ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2025/08/08/duolingos-pushy-paywall-play-smart-or-risky/)). The agent belongs inside the single Otto Club (ADR-0005).

## One action catalogue, one server loop, seventeen tools

### The loop runs in an Edge Function with the user's JWT

The agent is a new Edge Function, `otto-agent`. It calls OpenAI's **Responses API** (Chat Completions remains supported but is not recommended for new work, and the Assistants API shut down on 2026-08-26) with **strict function tools** ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/assistants-api-beta-deprecation-august-26-2026-sunset/1354666)). For each request it builds a Supabase client from the anon key plus the caller's forwarded `Authorization` header, so every tool runs under the caller's RLS ([Supabase: Securing Edge Functions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/auth.md)). This is OWASP LLM06's main mitigation, the user's own security context, and it needs no extra auth code ([Aembit on OWASP](https://aembit.io/blog/owasp-top-10-llm-risks-explained/)). `serviceClient()` must never appear in the agent ([`_shared/http.ts:36-41`](../../../supabase/functions/_shared/http.ts)). The loop is hand-rolled, about a hundred lines in the shape `while (function_call && steps < 6)`. Each round re-validates the arguments with zod, runs the tool, appends the output and calls again. A cap on steps and wall time protects the Edge runtime. Text deltas and card events stream to the client over the SSE frames Otto already parses ([`chat.queries.ts:112-184`](../../../src/features/chat/chat.queries.ts)). Anthropic's advice is to start with direct API calls because frameworks "can hide prompts and responses" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). Shopify found that one agent handles more than expected and that multi-agent setups should wait ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/building-production-ready-agentic-systems)). Both point to a hand-rolled loop over the Agents SDK, whose Deno compatibility is also untested. A third-party guide reports that strict mode and parallel calls conflict. Until OpenAI's docs confirm otherwise, set `parallel_tool_calls: false` and get batching from coarse tools that take arrays ([apiyi mirror](https://docs.apiyi.com/en/api-capabilities/openai/function-calling)).

Writes execute on the server, not on the phone. The alternative design returns tool calls to the app and lets the app run them through `usePlan()`, which gives optimistic UI for free. It loses for three reasons. It puts every loop round trip on the mobile network. It lets a modified client fabricate tool results, and the Agents SDK guidance says outright not to accept client-supplied tool calls or approval state ([Agents SDK HITL](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-js/guides/human-in-the-loop/)). And Siri and widgets will later need a path that does not depend on a running JS bundle. Device-native work comes back to the app as `client_actions[]`: start cook mode, schedule a local reminder, open the calendar subscription. The app runs these through the same catalogue, then invalidates the query keys named in the response's `changes[]` (`['plan', userId]`, `savedKey(userId)`, `['household-list', id]`; [`usePlan.ts:57`](../../../src/features/planner/usePlan.ts)).

### Every action is defined once and has an inverse

The seam already exists. Every durable action is a small typed function in a `*.queries.ts` file, and screens never import supabase-js directly ([`plan.queries.ts:1-2`](../../../src/features/planner/plan.queries.ts)). The change is to move each one into `src/shared/actions/` as `{ name, description, schema (zod), risk, runsOn, execute(client, userId, input), inverse }`. Because `execute` takes the Supabase client as a parameter, the same function body runs in React Native with the session client and in Deno with the forwarded-JWT client. `zod-to-json-schema` generates the OpenAI tool definitions from the same schemas. Edge Functions bundle from `supabase/functions/`, so the shared module is either copied there by a build step or imported through a path the bundler accepts. Either way, a test asserts that the two copies are identical. A second test fails whenever a UI mutation has no Action, and that test is what makes "anything you can do by hand, Otto can do" true and checkable. Each mutating call carries `idempotency_key = hash(response_id, call_id)` with a unique constraint in a new RLS table, `agent_actions(user_id, response_id, call_id, tool, args_redacted, status proposed|applied|undone|rejected, inverse jsonb, tokens, created_at)`. A model that re-emits a call therefore cannot double-add. Undo replays `inverse` through the same action. The same rows become the "Otto did" activity log in the Otto tab, with per-row undo for 24 hours. This satisfies Microsoft HAX pattern 9C, which says to show what an automated action changed and let the user revert it ([HAX 9C](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/pattern/g9-c-undo-automated-actions/)).

Shopify saw tool boundaries blur somewhere around **20–50 tools**, and its fix was "JIT instructions" returned inside tool results, which also keeps the prompt cache intact ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/building-production-ready-agentic-systems)). Otto stays well under that range. Tools take IDs returned by the read tools, never titles. Dates are ISO strings resolved in the user's timezone by the server, and servings are integers from 1 to 12. Anthropic calls this "poka-yoke" for tool arguments ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)).

Some things are never visible to the model at all: allergies and diet (editable only in Preferences), deleting a recipe, deleting the account, subscription and billing, household create/join/leave, and any whole-collection clear. Cook-mode commands such as "next step" and "timer 8 minutes" are not agent tools either. They run on a deterministic on-device grammar inside cook mode, cost nothing and need no consent. The tiers follow the practitioner consensus that approving every step trains reflexive approval. Act-then-undo covers cheap reversible changes. Interruptions are kept for bulk, outward-facing or sensitive actions ([setproduct](https://www.setproduct.com/blog/ai-agent-ui-design-patterns); [web.dev](https://web.dev/learn/ai/ux-patterns)). Risk 2 goes further than a model-side "confirm" step: the model can *propose* a week but cannot commit one, because the only path to `apply` is the user tapping the card's button. A spoken "yes, use it" is mapped by the client to that same visible button.

### Answers are cards, and cards act without the model

The agent returns `{ text?, cards[], client_actions[], changes[] }`. Card types are `week` (seven `PlanDayRow`s), `recipe` (the existing `RecipeCard`), `list_diff`, `action_receipt`, `meal_estimate` and `suggestion`. All of them are built from design-system components, so AI output looks like Otto rather than chat. Card buttons (Swap, Cook, Use this plan, Save) call `src/shared/actions` directly with no model round trip. The model writes the card, and after that it is ordinary UI. Context is a small block built on the server each turn: household size, diet and allergies as read-only hard constraints, a summary of this week's plan, and the last few turns. It sits behind a stable prefix (system prompt, then the tool schemas in sorted order, then the profile), so the prompt cache holds. `previous_response_id` carries the conversation forward without resending history ([Agents SDK: running agents](https://openai.github.io/openai-agents-js/guides/running-agents/)). Today the chat sends none of this context. `ChatScreen` calls `useChat` with no servings, diet or cuisines, even though the server already accepts those lines ([`ChatScreen.tsx:81`](../../../src/features/chat/ChatScreen.tsx); [`generate-recipe/index.ts:299-304`](../../../supabase/functions/generate-recipe/index.ts)).

### Six prerequisites in the code would come first

The code shows six blockers. Each one would fit Phase 0. **P1: preferences live only on the device** (kv `prefs`), so a server agent cannot enforce allergies. R0.4/R0.5 (the `profiles` table with `allergies[]`) must land first, and ADR-0010 moves from Proposed to Accepted. **P2: swap is not atomic.** It runs `removePlanEntry` and then `addPlanEntry` as two calls ([`usePlan.ts:99-103`](../../../src/features/planner/usePlan.ts)), so an interrupted agent could leave a hole. `put_in_plan` with `replace_item_id` becomes a single `UPDATE` of the recipe columns on the existing row. The migrations have no unique `(user_id, day)` constraint, so "replace" is keyed by item id, not by day. **P3: the personal shopping list is device-only state inside a 1,110-line screen.** `toggle`, `addCustom`, `removeItem` and the rest are callbacks persisted to kv `shoppingState`, and only households have a server table ([`ShoppingScreen.tsx:291-466`](../../../src/features/planner/ShoppingScreen.tsx); [`household.queries.ts:140-205`](../../../src/features/household/household.queries.ts)). Lift the callbacks into a `useShoppingList` hook, then generalise `household_list_state` into a list state owned by a user *or* a household. **P4: the rate limiter is an isolate-local `Map`** that resets on every cold start ([`_shared/http.ts:96-110`](../../../supabase/functions/_shared/http.ts)). A multi-step loop needs a Postgres counter of turns per day and steps per turn, and the code's own comment already names that fix. **P5: the club gate is off by default** (`REQUIRE_CLUB` must be `on`, and its production value is not in source control; [EDGE_FUNCTIONS.md](../../architecture/EDGE_FUNCTIONS.md)). `otto-agent` should call `requireClub` unconditionally. **P6: consent is per device** ([ADR-0006](../../adr/0006-ai-runs-on-openai-behind-supabase-edge-functions-with-per-pr.md)), so a Siri, widget or notification path has no consent sheet to show. Record consent on the account as well, have the function refuse requests without it, and keep `aiConsentGranted()` fail-closed on non-interactive paths. P6 would amend ADR-0006, and the design as a whole would need a new ADR, "AI acts on the user's data through one action catalogue" (a future ADR-0012, not written).

## Voice is a microphone on the same text path

Text is the primary input. The founder's "test" means typed requests, and voice is a way to fill the same composer and send the same request. That rules out a separate voice agent. Otto already ships dictation through `expo-speech-recognition` 3.1.3, but `requiresOnDeviceRecognition` is unset, so audio streams to Apple's servers and the privacy truth table lists Apple as an audio recipient ([`useSpeechInput.ts:5-15`](../../../src/features/chat/useSpeechInput.ts)). The cheapest upgrade settles the open founder question. Set `requiresOnDeviceRecognition: true` wherever `supportsOnDeviceRecognition()` is true. Seed `contextualStrings` with this week's recipe titles, day names and list items, so food words are recognised. Use `iosTaskHint: 'confirmation'` for short commands ([expo-speech-recognition](https://github.com/jamsch/expo-speech-recognition)). Where on-device recognition is not supported, the code either refuses or keeps the disclosed Apple fallback. On iOS 26, Apple's `SpeechAnalyzer` is fully on-device and in an independent July 2026 test beat Whisper Small on LibriSpeech (**2.12% vs 3.74% WER**). An Argmax benchmark on call audio found the opposite, so call the two roughly level ([Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260714-apple-speech-analyzer-benchmark/); [Argmax](https://www.argmaxinc.com/blog/apple-and-argmax)). It reaches React Native through Callstack's `@react-native-ai/apple`, but it lacked `contextualStrings`-style vocabulary biasing as of iOS 26.0 ([AI SDK RN Apple](https://ai-sdk.dev/v5/providers/community-providers/react-native-apple); [DEV](https://dev.to/simple_memo/ios-26s-speechanalyzer-on-a-live-mic-the-5-things-the-docs-dont-tell-you-2ng5)). It is an upgrade path, not v1. Whisper loses on both routes. Bundling WhisperKit adds hundreds of MB of model for no gain over Apple. Cloud `gpt-4o-mini-transcribe` costs about **$0.003 a minute**, roughly $0.075 per user a month, and sends raw audio to OpenAI, which Otto has never done and which would need a new 5.1.2(i) disclosure ([CostGoat](https://costgoat.com/pricing/openai-transcription)). Spoken replies use `expo-speech` (AVSpeechSynthesizer on the device, no permission). Otto speaks only when the request was spoken or cook mode is open, and a mute toggle is always available.

Siri arrives through App Intents, not through the agent. Expo now has an official `expo-app-intents` package, but it is **alpha with frequent breaking changes**, its docs reference SDK 58, and intents must be written in Swift ([Expo AppIntents](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/app-intents/)). Its JS handler receives queued invocations only when the app starts. An intent that answers without opening the app ("Hey Siri, what's for dinner in Otto") must therefore be pure Swift, reading a snapshot of tonight's plan that the app writes to an App Group. The LLM Siri with on-screen awareness slipped from iOS 26.4 to iOS 27, launched as a beta, English-first and not in the EU ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/11/apple-reportedly-pushing-back-gemini-powered-siri-features-beyond-ios-26-4/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/08/ios-27-and-siri-ai-release-date/)). Treat it as upside. The first intents are `WhatsForDinnerIntent` (dialog plus a snippet with Start cooking and Swap), `MarkCookedIntent`, `AddToListIntent(item)` and cook-mode `NextStepIntent`. iOS 26 `SnippetIntent` is the protocol for snippets with working buttons ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/snippetintent)). They map one-to-one onto catalogue actions 5, 8, 12 and the cook grammar, so the catalogue is Otto's verb list for Siri as well. Apple Foundation Models (a ~3B on-device model with `@Generable` tool calling) could parse short commands for free, but only on Apple Intelligence devices. Any fallback has to be an OpenAI call or a grammar, so it saves little and should not be the v1 parser ([createwithswift](https://createwithswift.com/exploring-the-foundation-models-framework/)). Realtime speech-to-speech is rejected for v1 because of cost (next sections). It is the candidate for a later "hands-free cook mode" premium feature, once usage justifies it.

## The photo moment: the model names the food, USDA counts it, the user confirms

The research makes the architecture clear. With a photo alone, general vision LLMs miss energy by about **30–36% MAPE** and underestimate large portions systematically. When ChatGPT-5 was given the ingredients *with amounts*, its error fell from about **30.5% to 13.9%**, and removing the photo while keeping the ingredients made results worse ([Fridolfsson et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41081011/); [Nutrients 17:3613](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17223613)). Depth sensing only improves calorie error from 26.1% to 16.5–18.8% on Nutrition5k ([CVPR 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Thames_Nutrition5k_Towards_Automatic_Nutritional_Understanding_of_Generic_Food_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)), so confirming portions buys most of the gain without LiDAR. MyFitnessPal Meal Scan and Lose It! Snap It both end in a confirm step ([MFP](https://blog.myfitnesspal.com/meal-scan/)). Otto's existing law, that nutrition is computed by the on-device USDA engine, is exactly the "hybrid approach" the NIH authors recommend ([Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/ai-calorie-tracking-apps-underestimate-calories-fat-nih-study-2026-476487)).

The pipeline has five steps. First, a new mode in `generate-recipe` (or a sibling `analyze-meal`) receives a downscaled image with EXIF stripped on the device. It returns strict JSON `{ dishGuess, components[{ name, usdaQuery, confidence, portion{low, mid, high, unit}, hiddenRisk[oil|butter|sauce|sugar] }] }` and **no kcal**. Second, the phone maps components to `usdaTable` keys and grams with `computeNutrition`, and `resolve-nutrition` handles misses. Unmatched items stay visible as "Not counted" chips. Third, Otto asks **at most two questions**, chosen by the largest kcal spread: the starch portion ("½ cup · 1 cup · 1½ cups") and "Cooked with oil or butter? None · a little · a lot". The fat question targets the documented failure. Fourth, the sheet shows `~540 kcal` with a range of 450–650, `~P 32 g · C 60 g · F 18 g` rounded to 10 kcal, an "Otto assumed" list where every line can be tapped, and the source line "Numbers from USDA FoodData Central. Photo read by OpenAI." Fifth, for a recipe the search goes **retrieval-first**: it matches `otto_recipes` and offers "Closest Otto recipe: Chicken burrito bowl · edit to match yours". It generates a fresh recipe only when nothing matches, labels it "Otto's guess at this dish" and opens it in the review-first editor. Retrieval-augmented recipe generation is the research answer to hallucinated recipes ([WACV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/papers/Liu_Retrieval_Augmented_Recipe_Generation_WACV_2025_paper.pdf)). Servings default to "1 serving as pictured", and when saving Otto asks how many servings the whole batch made rather than inferring it. Nutrition shown on the card and on the detail screen is then the same engine output, which is the "card and detail agree" law. At mini-class prices a scan costs an estimated **$0.001–0.002**, or 5–10× that on `gpt-5.1`. Identification may be possible on a mini model, but only after Otto calibrates it on its own small weighed test set ([OpenAI vision guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision)).

The feature is framed as cooking, not as dieting. Among 105 people with eating disorders, **74.3% used MyFitnessPal and 73.1% of those said it contributed to their symptoms** (self-reported). Tracking for weight or shape reasons carried more risk ([Levinson 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319194941_My_Fitness_Pal_calorie_tracker_usage_in_the_eating_disorders); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1471015321000957)). The photo entry is therefore "Snap a dish → save it as a recipe" in the Add sheet (R6.2) and in the Otto tab. Otto never keeps a running daily total, streak, red/green budget or "bad food" label, offers a setting to hide numbers, and never prompts the user to photograph every meal. The copy never claims "accurate" or quotes a percentage, because Apple 1.4.1 asks health-measurement apps to disclose methodology and the FTC has closed an AI health app over a 94.4% accuracy claim ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/703502); [FTC](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/hehealth-closing-letter.pdf)). HealthKit writing is out of v1. Cook mode's existing "Snap your plate" stays a journal photo with no model call.

## Cards you can press, and an Otto that mostly stays quiet

Swipes and long-presses have no visual affordance, so every card action needs a visible control and gestures are only accelerators ([GhostUI, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19258)). Each card calls the same catalogue action as Otto, which keeps the manual path and the AI path one code path. That makes R4.2 (the dish actions sheet) the natural home for the card spec.

| Card | Visible control | Long-press menu (iOS context menu) | Swipe accelerator | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tonight (Home, R3.2) | Start cooking · Swap · Cooked it (R5.6) | Swap · Move to… · Leftovers tomorrow · Remove · Add to list | none (the hero card does not swipe) | Haptic and toast "Swapped · Undo" |
| Plan day row (R4.1) | Tap → recipe; ⋯ → actions sheet | Cooked · Swap · Move to… · Leftovers · Remove | Leading swipe: Cooked; trailing: Remove (both undoable) | One-time peek animation teaches the swipe |
| Empty day | "Pick a meal" (Saved · Discover · Ask Otto, R4.3) | n/a | n/a | "Open. No plans, no guilt." stays |
| Swap sheet | Three candidates that obey allergies, each with Use this | n/a | Horizontal deck of suggestions (accept/skip) is fine here, not on the plan | Reduce Motion respected |
| Agent cards (week preview, recipe, list diff) | Their own primary button (Use this plan · Save · Add all) | Same actions as the equivalent manual card | Same as manual | Receipt card links to "Otto did" |

Moving a dinner needs a target day, so drag-and-drop between days is a later enhancement over "Move to…", and a Tinder-style swipe belongs only on a deck of suggestions ([Dolfy](https://www.dolfy.ai/blog/swipe-to-delete-gesture-discoverability-mobile-apps)). Todoist's pattern of highlighting parsed dates that the user can tap to revert fits typed requests well. "tacos Thursday for 4" shows Thursday and 4 as chips before the agent sends ([Todoist](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-dates-and-time-q7VobO)).

Proactive Otto follows one rule set, which these notes imply rather than cite. **The trigger is code, the content comes from the catalogue, and AI runs only when the user taps.** A suggestion card therefore costs nothing, needs no consent and cannot hallucinate. Otto speaks up in five situations. Tonight is empty at the user's reminder hour ("Pick tonight: three 20-minute dinners"). Sunday's planning slot arrives with empty days (the existing Sunday nudge, plus "Fill my empty days"). A cooked dinner made more servings than the household eats ("Leftovers tomorrow?"). The week has dinners but the list has never been opened before the first planned day. A saved recipe has gone unplanned for three weeks. It stays quiet in five situations. A suggestion of that kind was dismissed this week (one dismissal silences the kind for seven days). More than one suggestion would be on screen (at most one card on Home). Cook mode is open. The time falls outside the user's chosen hours. Reminders are off, because push stays opt-in and the existing notifications default to off ([`useNotifPrefs.ts:13-64`](../../../src/features/notifications/useNotifPrefs.ts)). Microsoft HAX warns that correction costs can exceed doing the task by hand, which argues for this restraint ([HAX 9C](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/pattern/g9-c-undo-automated-actions/)). Every suggestion carries a visible "Not now".

## Widgets, Live Activities and the calendar arrive cheapest-first

Otto has no widget, App Intent, Live Activity or calendar code, no `ios/` folder and no target plugin today ([`app.json:94-121`](../../../app.json)). Every native surface needs a Swift target. On SDK 54 the working route is `@bacons/apple-targets`, which is "highly experimental" and shares state through App Group `ExtensionStorage`. `expo-widgets` is alpha in SDK 55 ([GitHub](https://github.com/EvanBacon/expo-apple-targets); [Expo blog](https://expo.dev/blog/home-screen-widgets-and-live-activities-in-expo)). Interactive widgets run App Intents from buttons without opening the app on iOS 17+, iOS 18 controls bring them to Control Center and the Action button, and Live Activities last about **8 hours, plus up to 4 on the Lock Screen**. A cook timer fits comfortably within that ([Swiftjectivec](https://swiftjectivec.com/snip-create-a-basic-interactive-widget-using-app-intent-button); [Apple: controls](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/widgetkit/creating-controls-to-perform-actions-across-the-system.md); [Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/797676)).

| Order | Surface | Route on Expo SDK 54 | Swift? | Ticket |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notification actions "Cooked it" / "Swap" plus deep links | expo-notifications categories; today's notifications carry only `{title, body}` | No | R8.2 |
| 2 | Calendar: "Subscribe in Calendar" ICS feed | Edge Function `plan-ics` returning `text/calendar` under a per-user revocable token; `webcal://` link | No | proposed R7.x |
| 3 | Calendar: one-off "Add tonight to Calendar" | `expo-calendar` `addEventWithForm` (system editor, write-only key) | No | with 2 |

The ICS feed beats EventKit writes because write-only access cannot read back or update what it wrote, while a subscription stays in sync as the plan changes and works in Google and Outlook too. That is how Plan to Eat does it ([Apple TN3152](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3152-migrating-to-the-latest-calendar-access-levels.md); [Plan to Eat](https://learn.plantoeat.com/help/share-your-meal-plan-to-your-calendar-app)). Treat the token like a secret. Events carry only "Dinner: Lemon chicken" plus a deep link, never allergens or household data. Google refresh is slow, so Otto should not promise "instant". The founder asked for breakfast, lunch and dinner, and today **`plan_entries` has no meal slot**: Otto plans dinners, and the North Star counts dinners. The recommended move, when work starts, is a cheap first migration, `meal text not null default 'dinner'` with a check constraint, which makes the ICS feed, the agent schema and the widgets future-proof. The feed exports dinners as timed events at the user's dinner hour. Breakfast and lunch planning UI waits until dinner activation is proven, because a three-slot week dilutes "Tonight is answered" and the catalogue is built around its 510 dinners ([ALLERGEN_COVERAGE.md](../../product/ALLERGEN_COVERAGE.md)). Interactive widget buttons come last for a specific reason. A Swift intent that writes to Supabase needs the session token in shared storage, and ADR-0011 forbids queueing mutations offline. The first widget therefore deep-links into the app for actions, and only a spike decides how a widget writes.

## Safety, App Review and cost are design inputs

The prompt-injection risk is real and bounded. Willison's "lethal trifecta" is private data, untrusted content and external communication together ([Willison](https://simonw.substack.com/p/the-lethal-trifecta-for-ai-agents)). Otto has the first two (household, allergies, imported pages) and deliberately lacks the third. The agent gets **no `fetch_url`, share or email tool**. Imports already run as a fixed pipeline, and should become a **tool-less, schema-only extraction call** whose output reaches the agent only as structured fields, never as raw page text. This is the Dual-LLM and context-minimisation pattern: "once an LLM agent has ingested untrusted input, it must be constrained so that it is impossible for that input to trigger any consequential actions" ([Willison on design patterns](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/13/prompt-injection-design-patterns/)). Allergies are enforced inside `search_recipes`, `plan_week` and `put_in_plan` in SQL or code, and no `ignore_allergies` parameter exists. A blocked add returns `blocked: allergen <x>`, and the app renders that as a system-styled warning the model cannot reword ([ALLERGEN_COVERAGE.md](../../product/ALLERGEN_COVERAGE.md)). An eval set of about 100 scripted requests runs in CI against a Supabase branch on every prompt, schema or model change. It covers moves, week plans, injection-laden imports and allergen conflicts, and scores DB end state, tool and argument accuracy, and the unnecessary-confirm rate. Two metrics must be zero: allergen violations and unintended mutations.

App Review imposes four requirements. Guideline **5.1.2(i)** now names third-party AI and requires explicit permission before personal data is shared ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)). Otto's consent sheet must therefore name OpenAI and list messages and spoken requests (as text), meal photos, the household and diet profile, and plan and recipes. It appears before the first AI call on every entry point. Reviewers have missed consent screens, so the review notes (R10.4) say exactly where it appears ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/820209)). Guideline **1.2** now covers "AI content creation apps" and requires filtering, reporting and blocking ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The agent therefore gets a moderation pass on input and output, a "Report this response" action on AI cards writing to an `ai_reports` table, and a visible support contact. Guideline **1.4.1** is met by the USDA source line and the no-accuracy-claims copy. For the **age rating**, Apple says developers "must consider how all app features, including AI assistants and chatbot functionality, impact" sensitive content ([Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ks775ehf)). Answer that AI is present, keep the assistant scoped to food, and frame allergies as preferences, not medical advice. `APP_PRIVACY_TRUTH_TABLE.md` gains photos and user content sent to OpenAI. Audio stays with Apple, or off the network entirely once recognition runs on the device.

## Decisions to make before any build

Six calls are the founder's, and the plan above assumes the recommended answer to each. First, whether server-executed writes and the action catalogue become a decision record (the future ADR-0012), with ADR-0010 accepted ahead of it. Second, whether AI consent is also recorded on the account (an ADR-0006 amendment). Third, whether to flip dictation to on-device recognition now, which settles the open question in `useSpeechInput.ts`. Fourth, whether the photo moment ships as a recipe feature with a hide-numbers setting, or not at all. Fifth, whether `plan_entries` gains a `meal` column while Otto stays a dinner planner. Sixth, when to spike the Expo SDK upgrade that App Intents and widgets probably need, because Otto is on SDK 54 and the official packages are alpha on later SDKs. All OpenAI prices and model names need checking on OpenAI's own pages before any of them enter a budget.

## Conclusion

The AI question turns out to be an architecture question first. Otto already writes every durable action as a small typed function under RLS. Turning those functions into one catalogue with inverses makes "Otto can do anything you can do" a test that passes or fails, not a slogan. The same catalogue then serves Siri and widgets almost for free, and keeps the AI from doing anything a tap could not. The proposed order follows from that: the Phase 0 plumbing (prefs on the account, atomic swap, a list on the server, a durable limiter, logged actions) comes before any new model capability. Every one of those items also improves the manual app.

The reframing for the magic moment is that AI's value in Otto shows up as **an undo button nobody presses**. Undo rate, allergen violations at zero and dinners cooked from Otto's changes are what show the AI is good. Chat volume shows nothing. The photo moment is worth building for delight and sharing, but it earns its place only when the snapped dish becomes a planned and cooked dinner. That is why it is a recipe feature with honest ranges and should never become a calorie counter.
