# Build Otto's three moments on today's platform

Status: draft · planning only · nothing here is built, scheduled or ticketed · Last verified against code at commit 607e37f4 · 2026-11-05 · Research synthesis from the notes in `docs/learning/research_notes/Otto three magic moments plan/`, building on `Otto AI agent research.md` and `Otto magic moment research.md`. Ticket numbers below (X1, X2, …) are discussion numbers only, not catalogue entries. OpenAI prices come from third-party trackers, and every cost is an **estimate** until checked against OpenAI's own pages.

Build the three magic moments as **one loop on the platform Otto already has, not as a rewrite**. You snap a dish, and Otto writes the whole recipe for it into your cookbook. Otto then plans that recipe into your week, you cook it, and what you kept, swapped and cooked teaches Otto what to plan next. **Snap any dish sells the trial**: it happens before the account (ADR-0013, option A), it gives a result in one photo, and nobody at scale does it honestly. **Otto plans your week keeps the subscriber**: it is the weekly job that brings people back and feeds the North Star, planned items cooked. **Otto AI doing anything you can do by hand** is the multiplier that turns both into a conversation, and the existing agent design already covers it with small changes. The repository evidence argues against starting from scratch. The nutrition engine and its golden corpus, the 795-recipe canonical catalogue with per-line grams and USDA keys, RLS, auth, RevenueCat, the review-first save flow and the design system are sound and tested. Only two pieces are true rebuilds: the vision prompt, which today transcribes recipe pages and declines plates, and the chat brain, which has no tools. The real work is new: a dish-reconstruction pipeline, a yield and serving-unit model, plan items with kinds, labels and order, a hybrid planner, the agent, and a pre-account scan path. PRODUCT_ROADMAP's release order (1.0.24 → Foundations → 1.1 Snap → 1.2 Plan → 1.3 Everywhere) holds. It needs one new Foundations lane, **"recipe model v2"** (yield, serving unit, ranges, forks, media, cook events), plus three blockers made explicit: the catalogue flag is off in production code by default, the pre-account identity mechanism is undecided, and no calibration set exists yet.

## One loop: the photo sells the trial, the week keeps the member

The three moments are one flywheel, and each step feeds the next. A **snap** produces a whole-batch recipe with honest numbers, saved in the cookbook. The **cookbook** is now a candidate pool the planner treats as first-class, which answers the most specific complaint about Lifesum's planner: it "only works for Lifesum's recipes, not user-added ones" ([AppsHunter Lifesum reviews](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/286906691/reviews/5)). The **planner** puts that recipe on Thursday, and its leftovers on Friday's lunch. **Cooking** it creates a cook event, which closes the North Star loop and gives the user a moment to replace any AI image with a real photo (ADR-0014). **Learning** comes from what the user kept, swapped, undid and cooked, and it makes next week's plan better. Otto AI runs through every step, because each step is also an action in one catalogue that the user can tap or say.

| Moment | Job in the business | Why | Number that proves it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Snap any dish | **Acquisition and trial start.** Shown before the account | One photo gives a concrete, personal and shareable result. Competitors either stop at calories (Cal AI and others) or are thin GPT wrappers with no ratings | `dish_snapped` → `recipe_from_photo_saved`; scan → trial against the 1.0.24 baseline; edits per scan |
| 2 Otto plans your week | **Retention.** The weekly habit | "What's for dinner" recurs every week. Planners that work become routine, and the plan feeds cooking | `week_planned_by_otto` → `item_cooked`; planned items cooked per household per week (North Star) |
| 3 Otto does anything | **Depth and stickiness.** It lowers the cost of every edit | Editing a week by hand is the chore that kills planners. "Swap Thursday, I've got chicken thighs" removes it | First Otto action kept (not undone within 10 minutes); undo rate |

The ranking matters for sequencing. Snap must be excellent at launch because it is the first impression. Planning must be excellent by the end of the first trial week, because that is when the renewal decision forms. The roadmap already puts them in that order. One correction to the earlier agent report: that report kept "Tonight is answered" (no AI) as moment 1 and called the photo "the wrong first impression" because photo-calorie apps undercount by about a third ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260726015237.htm)). ADR-0013 overrides that ranking. The way to honour the underlying concern is the design below. Otto never shows a model's calorie guess. It shows a recipe whose fats are explicit, editable lines and whose numbers come from USDA arithmetic, with a range.

One live contradiction needs fixing in the planning docs. PRODUCT_ROADMAP §5 lists "AI before the account or paywall" as deliberately *not* on the roadmap, while ADR-0013 (Accepted) puts the scan before the account. The ADR wins under the authority table in CLAUDE.md, but the roadmap line should be corrected when the roadmap is next edited. That edit is outside this planning-only report.

## Snap any dish: a seven-stage pipeline where the model writes words and code writes numbers

### What exists, and what is genuinely new

"Photo of a cooked dish → full recipe" exists only in small single-purpose App Store apps with few or no ratings: Recipix, DishDecoder, Recipe Lens AI, BiteFrame, Recipe Scanner AI and others ([Recipix](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/recipix-ai-recipe-generator/id6502718973); [DishDecoder](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dishdecoder/id6742336211); [AI Get Recipe By Photo, 1 rating](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6475171661)). Samsung Food announced dish recognition at IFA 2023 but shipped *ingredient* recognition for a pantry list in 2024 ([Samsung Newsroom 2023](https://news.samsung.com/global/meet-samsung-food-at-ifa-2023-the-ultimate-personalized-cooking-assistant); [Samsung UK 2024](https://news.samsung.com/uk/upgraded-samsung-food-raises-the-bar-for-food-experiences-at-ifa-2024)). The big recipe managers (ReciMe, Flavorish, Honeydew) do OCR of printed recipes, which is what Otto's vision mode does today ([ReciMe](https://www.recime.app/smart-imports-explained); [Flavorish](https://www.flavorish.ai/blog/save-recipes-from-an-image-with-flavorish)). Calorie apps estimate a plate and measurably undercount: in an NIH analysis of 102 weighed meals, MyFitnessPal, LoseIt!, Cal AI and Appediet all came in **about 250–345 kcal low per meal, and about 30 g of fat low** ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260726015237.htm); [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/photo-based-calorie-tracking-apps-may-underestimate-energy-in-meals)).

Three things are new in Otto's version. The research found no prior art for any of them. First, Otto **infers the whole batch from a portion**: two slices become a loaf recipe served by the slice. Second, it **computes nutrition deterministically** from USDA rows instead of asking the model. Third, it **turns the camera's blind spot, hidden fat, into an editable line** ("2 tbsp olive oil · estimated") instead of a silent error. The third is worth saying out loud in product copy, because fat is exactly what the NIH study found cameras miss. The research also found no benchmark that scores amounts, yield or baking chemistry. Otto's accuracy therefore has to come from its own calibration set, not from a paper.

### The pipeline

The literature points to one shape. Image-only generation is poor: in LLaVA-Chef's ablation, images alone scored CIDEr ≈ 0.004, and the food name plus ingredients was essential ([LLaVA-Chef](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.16889v1)). Large multimodal models write fluent recipes "but tend to hallucinate", and the reported fix is to retrieve similar known recipes into the prompt ([Liu et al., WACV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/html/Liu_Retrieval_Augmented_Recipe_Generation_WACV_2025_paper.html)). Two-stage designs (identify, then write) run through the literature and the shipping apps ([Inverse Cooking](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Salvador_Inverse_Cooking_Recipe_Generation_From_Food_Images_CVPR_2019_paper.html); [Meal42](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6757866043)). On visible ingredients, GPT-4o-class models reach **F1 0.74–0.86** ([Pic2Plate](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11768646/); [January Food Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09966v1)). No public evaluation of GPT-5.1 on dish → recipe was found.

| # | Stage | Who | Output | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Identify** | gpt-5-mini (or gpt-5.1, low reasoning) with the image | `{dish_name, alternates[3], archetype, components[], visible_portion {count, unit, g_range}, hidden_cues[], confidence}`, e.g. `banana bread · loaf · 2 slice` | No calories in the schema. Streams the first reveal in about 2–5 s **[estimate]** |
| 2 | **Confirm chips** | User, one screen | Dish name (alternates as chips) · yield chip `slices per loaf [8 · 10 · 12]` · at most 2 "can't see" chips ("nuts?", "cooked in oil or butter?") | Fixes the largest error class at the cost of one tap. Matches ADR-0013's "at most 2 questions" |
| 3 | **Retrieve** | Code | Top 3–5 `otto_recipes` by name and ingredients, each with yield and serving unit | New: no catalogue search exists today beyond category/area filters and title search |
| 4 | **Reconstruct** | gpt-5.1, text only, JSON schema | Whole-batch recipe; each ingredient carries an **Otto ingredient key** from `usdaTable`, grams and a `confidence`; own step wording; `based_on_recipe_id` when one retrieved recipe dominates | Free-text items outside the table go through `resolve-nutrition`, where the model only *picks* among USDA fdcIds |
| 5 | **Code checks** | Code | Pass, or fail with the reason | Baker's percentages for baked archetypes; total mass against the archetype; grams per serving against the FNDDS portion weight (flag if more than about 35% off); kcal per serving above 1000 triggers review. On failure, regenerate **once** with the failed check stated, then show "estimate" badges |
| 6 | **Nutrition** | On-device USDA engine | Per serving and per portion, `~` with a range | Never from the model. The range comes from the engine's existing `confidence`/`doubt` plus the portion uncertainty |
| 7 | **Yield and serving logic** | Code | "Banana bread · **Makes 1 loaf (10 slices)** · You photographed 2 slices ≈ 2 servings · ~390 kcal (~330–470)" | Recipe and plate are two separate lines. The recipe is never stored as "2 slices" |

The baker's-percentage guard rails are the cheapest accuracy win, because they catch classic LLM baking errors such as a tablespoon of baking soda in one loaf. The starting ranges for quick breads (flour = 100%) come from domain knowledge, not a source, and need checking against the catalogue's baked recipes before use: liquid including banana and egg 60–110%, sugar 30–80%, fat 20–60%, about 1–2% baking soda equivalent or 3–5% baking powder, and salt 0.5–2%. The same applies to the archetype yield table (loaf 10 slices, 9-inch pie 8, 9-inch round cake 12, 9×13 casserole 12, cookies 24, muffins 12, pizza 8, soup pot 6 bowls, stir-fry or pasta 4, composed plate 1). Retrieval should supply the yield first, with the table only as a fallback. Before it seeds anything, compute the real distributions from `otto_recipes` (discussion ticket **X3**).

The yield arithmetic rests on three separate quantities: **yield** (what the recipe makes: 1 loaf, about 900 g cooked), **servings** (what it divides into: 10, by the slice) and **portion eaten** (what was on the plate: 2 slices). Every serious tool reduces a serving to a gram weight. USDA FoodData Central stores nutrients per 100 g plus `foodPortions` rows ("1 slice" = N g) ([FNDDS documentation](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/80400530/pdf/fndds/2019_2020_FNDDS_Doc.pdf)). MyFitnessPal and Cronometer users who lack a cooked-weight field fall back to logging "223 servings" of a 1 g serving ([MFP community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/comment/48703381); [Cronometer support](https://support.cronometer.com/hc/en-us/articles/360018510311)). The portion is the least reliable term. A "slice of banana bread" ranges from **40 g to 92 g** across sources ([Foodstruct](https://foodstruct.com/food/banana-bread/1-oz); [FatSecret](https://www.fatsecret.com.au/calories-nutrition/generic/banana-bread); [UMaine](https://extension.umaine.edu/publications/?p=17789)). So the "as eaten" number from a photo always carries an estimate tag, even when the recipe's own nutrition is fully computed. Label law sets a different number: the FDA reference amount for sweet quick breads is 55 g ([eCFR 101.12](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-101/subpart-A/section-101.12)). Otto may show it beside a serving but must never divide by it.

### Before the account: consent, the device limit and the photo

Option A puts the scan before the account. That has three consequences the code does not handle today. **Consent** naming OpenAI must come before the upload, because Apple's guideline 5.1.2(i) (revised 13 November 2025) requires explicit permission before personal data goes to third-party AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/13/apples-new-app-review-guidelines-clamp-down-on-apps-sharing-personal-data-with-third-party-ai)). The flow is Welcome → "Snap any dish" → consent sheet → camera, never camera first. **Identity** is the second consequence. Every AI endpoint requires a user JWT before anything else, the onboarding gate sends users without a session only to sign-in, and no anonymous sign-in or DeviceCheck code exists. There are two options. Supabase anonymous auth gives the device a real (anonymous) uid, so RLS, rate limits and later claim-on-sign-up work unchanged; the risk is anonymous-user sprawl and abuse. A separate unauthenticated `scan-dish` endpoint verified by App Attest/DeviceCheck is narrower, but it is new auth code outside RLS. This report recommends anonymous auth *plus* App Attest on the scan call. The anonymous uid makes the drafts claimable when the user creates an account, and attestation stops scripted abuse "the way `canonicalize` can today". Either way, the **durable Postgres rate limiter (R0.11) must ship before the endpoint**, because today's limiter is an isolate-local `Map` that resets on cold start. **The photo** is the third consequence. The memo says photos are "not kept on the server", yet the user's photo should become the recipe image. Both hold if the photo is sent for analysis without being stored, kept on the device as a draft, and uploaded only on Save, after the account exists. The upload should go to a **private** bucket or path. Today's `recipe-photos` bucket is public, which is wrong for photos of people's dinners.

Apple's guideline 1.4.1 adds scrutiny for apps whose health data could be inaccurate, and it asks apps to disclose the methodology behind accuracy claims ([ConductAtlas 1.4.1 index](https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/provision/CA-P-018796/medical-apps-with-inaccurate-data-may-be-rejected/)). So snap nutrition carries the label "Estimated from a recipe Otto wrote from your photo · USDA FoodData Central", with a "How Otto estimates" sheet. Store copy never says "accurate calories".

### Accuracy calibration is the ship gate

No benchmark measures amounts, yield or whether a reconstructed recipe works when cooked. Otto needs its own **calibration set** (discussion ticket **X1**) before 1.1 ships, as PRODUCT_ROADMAP already requires. The notes size it at **30–50 home-cooked dishes with known recipes**, photographed as served portions, deliberately including the expected failure classes. Those classes, inferred rather than benchmarked: invisible fats and sugars, sauces and stocks, salt and spice amounts, look-alikes (banana vs zucchini vs pumpkin loaf), baking chemistry, garnish counted as an ingredient, and side dishes merged into the main. Score each run on six measures: dish-name accuracy (top-1 and top-3), ingredient F1, gram error on the five largest ingredients, yield error, and kcal-per-serving error against the true recipe run through Otto's *own* engine. The last measure isolates reconstruction error from database error. The set becomes a CI-style regression suite for every prompt or model change, the way the golden nutrition corpus already is. Ship thresholds are a founder decision (gate **F-2** below). A reasonable starting point to argue about is: top-3 dish name correct in about 90% of cases after the confirm chip, and median kcal-per-serving error within the displayed range.

### Cost, latency and IP

At third-party-reported GPT-5.1 prices ($1.25 per million input tokens, $10 per million output, with reasoning billed as output), a one-call scan costs about **$0.03–0.04**, with a worst case near $0.07 when a failed check forces a regeneration ([pricepertoken](https://pricepertoken.com/pricing-page/model/openai-gpt-5.1)). The two-step design costs about the same in total, but its identify step costs **under $0.002** on gpt-5-mini. That matters because three free pre-account scans per device then cost roughly **$0.10–0.15 per device** in model fees, and abandoned scans that stop at the chip screen cost almost nothing. These figures are **higher than the memo's "$0.002–0.02 per scan"**, which should be corrected before unit economics are modelled. Re-check against OpenAI's own pricing page, which this session could not reach. Latency is unmeasured. Plan for 8–25 s end to end, hidden by staging the reveal: name and chips at about 2–5 s, the recipe card after writing, and nutrition instantly on the device once the ingredients arrive. Measure the real numbers from the existing `generate-recipe` vision logs before fixing UX timings.

On IP, US law treats "a mere listing of ingredients … or a simple set of directions" as uncopyrightable, but protects "substantial literary expression" and compilations ([Copyright Office Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf); [FL-122](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130103070723/http://www.copyright.gov/fls/fl122.pdf)). Otto may therefore adapt a retrieved recipe's ingredients and method as facts. It must write its own step prose, never copy headnotes, and show "Based on Otto's Banana Bread" (or "Inspired by <source>") when one retrieved recipe dominates. Catalogue-only retrieval keeps this simple. Web retrieval would add licensing and cost questions that were not researched, so leave it out of 1.1. The user's real photo is the image, so ADR-0014's generated image never applies to a snap.

## Otto plans your week: code decides what is safe, the model decides what you meant

### Why hybrid, with evidence

Pure-LLM plans look plausible but miss targets and occasionally break hard constraints. When ChatGPT planned diets for allergic personas, **4 of 56 (7.1%) contained a forbidden allergen**, for example almond milk in a nut-free diet ([Niszczota & Rybicka, PubMed 37269717](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37269717/)). Dietitian reviews of GPT-4o menus found macro and micronutrient shortfalls ([PubMed 40111941](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40111941/)). The 2023 allergen figure is the newest one available, and no 2026 GPT-5-class evaluation was found. Optimisation satisfies constraints but feels impractical. In a 2026 medRxiv preprint, a filter → MILP top-10 → LLM-chooses hybrid beat standalone MILP on practicality and personalisation, while MILP alone scored best on nutrient accuracy (4.93) and variety ([medRxiv 2026.02.14.26346312](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.02.14.26346312v1.full-text)). LAPPI reaches the same split from the other side: the LLM translates the request into a solver instance, because "LLMs alone cannot reliably enforce constraints" ([LAPPI, arXiv 2512.14138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14138)). With 795 catalogue recipes plus the user's own, no budget targets and only opt-in nutrition, a full MILP is overkill. A **greedy or beam search over a scoring function** gives the same feasible-by-construction guarantee, and it makes "swap just this one" fast.

| Step | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Interpret | LLM, structured output | Utterance + profile → typed `PlanRequest`: date range, which labels to fill, servings, per-day overrides ("Tuesday vegetarian", "Friday eating out"), max minutes, "fewer dishes" → leftovers allowed, exclusions |
| 2 Filter | Code, authoritative | Allergens (from reviewed tags on ingredient keys), diet, dislikes, blocked recipes, label fit. **Nothing the model says can loosen this** |
| 3 Score and assemble | Code, seeded and deterministic | Candidates = catalogue **plus the user's recipes and snaps**. Score = preference (favourites, ratings, cooked history, kept vs swapped) + variety (no repeat within N days, caps on protein and cuisine repeats) + reuse of perishable ingredient keys + leftovers after a dinner of 4 or more servings + time fit. Same seed → same plan; Regenerate changes the seed |
| 4 Gap fill | LLM, rare | Only where a slot has zero feasible candidates. The recipe goes through the canonicaliser and the same allergen check, is marked AI-written, and gets an ADR-0014 image |
| 5 Explain | LLM | One short reason per chosen item ("uses the rest of Monday's coriander"), written only about items code already chose |
| 6 Preview → commit | User tap | Week card; one tap commits the whole plan in one transaction with one Undo |

The model never emits a recipe id that was not in the candidate list, and never decides allergen safety. That puts PRODUCT_ROADMAP's split in the right place. 1.1's "Otto plans my week (deterministic)" is steps 2–3 and 6 with form inputs. 1.2 adds steps 1, 4 and 5 and the conversation.

### Full-day coverage, free lists and leftovers

ADR-0013 makes a day an ordered free list with optional labels, and the data model should follow the presentation exactly: **labels are presentation and filtering, never structure**. A full day needs more than recipes. Breakfast and snacks are mostly not recipes, and successful planners treat them that way. Eat This Much plans and swaps individual foods, and Lifesum users accept the same breakfast every day ([Eat This Much how-to](https://www.eatthismuch.com/how-to/); [AppsHunter Lifesum](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/286906691/reviews/5)). Plan items therefore get a `kind`: `recipe`, `leftover` (pointing at a cook event, adding nothing to the shopping list), `food` ("Greek yoghurt + berries", "apple", with an optional ingredient key and quantity so nutrition can still sum), `note` ("pizza night") and `eat_out`. By default the planner fills breakfast with a repeatable `food` item or a short breakfast recipe. It adds snacks only when the user has used them or asks. It leaves 5–7 dinners, not 7, when the household has room for leftovers and takeout ([Nori guide](https://heynori.com/blog/family-operations-system/ai-meal-planning-families-guide)). Leftovers follow Plan to Eat's and Member Kitchens' shipped pattern: a linked copy on a later day that contributes nothing to the list ([Plan to Eat](https://learn.plantoeat.com/doc/adding-leftovers-to-the-meal-planner); [Member Kitchens](https://docs.memberkitchens.com/en/articles/15461036-leftovers-and-prep-sessions)). This answers the PlateJoy complaint that cooking for one leaves "a crazy amount of leftovers" ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/platejoy-2/reviews)). The 3–4 day food-safety window gives the "eat by Thursday" hint ([safefood](https://www.safefood.net/Healthy-Eating/How-to-plan-your-meals/Meal-planning)). `food` items carry only a keyword allergen check, not the reviewed-tag guarantee, and the UI should say so.

### Editing: two primitives users already know, plus a diff

Every shipping planner converges on a per-meal shuffle that "won't impact the rest of your day" plus a regenerate-the-day/week button ([Eat This Much tutorial](https://blog.eatthismuch.com/eat-this-much-tutorial-3-getting-meals-you-like-part-1-how-to-regenerate-meals-favorite-and-block-foods/)). Otto's conversation maps onto exactly these primitives, plus list moves: `swap(item)`, `regenerate(day|week, keep_locked)`, `add(day, label?, recipe|food|note)`, `remove`, `move(item, day, position)`, `mark_leftovers(item, from_cook_event)`, `set_day_constraint(day, …)` and `label(item, label)`. "Make Tuesday vegetarian" becomes a constraint plus a regenerate of Tuesday that keeps locked items. "Something in 30 minutes" becomes a constraint plus a swap. Swipe, tap and voice call the same functions. Each edit returns a diff with Undo, and **swaps and undos are logged as implicit negative feedback, keeps and cooks as positive**. HelloFresh ranks menus from past choices and cart edits, though that detail rests on employee posts ([HelloFresh newsroom](https://hellofreshgroup.com/de/newsroom/stories/how-tech-and-big-data-enable-us-to-personalize-the-meal-kit-experience)). No study was found measuring how accurately LLMs map multi-turn utterances to plan edits. That accuracy belongs in the 1.2 eval set.

### Personalisation, cold start and the eating-disorder line

Cold start uses content, not collaborative filtering. Gousto uses recipe embeddings for exactly this ([Gousto engineering](https://medium.com/gousto-engineering-techbrunch/gousto-r-series-vol-2-tackling-the-cold-start-problem-in-recipe-recommendation-engine-af92a434805f)). Otto's first-week signals are the onboarding answers, the first snaps (a snapped banana bread says a lot), saves and the first swaps. "Otto knows the person" means a server-side profile the agent reads every turn: household, diet and allergies as read-only hard constraints, plus learned weights. Calories and macros stay **available but opt-in**. The planner never sets a calorie target unless the user asks for one, and the app makes no weight-loss defaults or medical claims. In one survey, 73.1% of MyFitnessPal users with eating disorders said the app contributed at least somewhat to their disorder (self-report, not causal). The design guidance from that work is to replace purely numeric tracking with non-numeric methods ([Eikey 2021, PMC8485346](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8485346/table/tab03)). This is also why the snap's "you ate" line should stay a by-product of the recipe, not the start of a food diary (gate **F-6**).

### The hard prerequisite is data, not AI

"30 minutes", "weekday vs weekend" and "breakfast-suitable" cannot be honoured without data: **0 of 795 catalogue recipes have any time field**, and meal-type facets beyond `category` are unmeasured. R0.14 (cook-time estimates, stored with `~`, reviewed sample) and a facet pass (course, diet and allergen tags derived from ingredient keys with the triggering keys stored) are on the critical path for 1.2, not polish. So is R0.4/R0.5, preferences with allergies on the account, because a server planner cannot enforce allergies that live in the phone's AsyncStorage.

## Otto AI does anything: the agent design stands, with six changes

The agent design in `Otto AI agent research.md` still holds. It is one `otto-agent` Edge Function running a hand-rolled Responses API tool loop capped at 6 steps, with the user's own JWT so RLS governs AI writes exactly like taps. One typed action catalogue (`src/shared/actions`) serves the UI, the agent, Siri and widgets, with a test that fails when a UI mutation has no action. Tools are tiered into act-then-undo, preview-then-tap and confirm. `agent_actions` stores idempotency and undo. Answers are cards that act without the model. Voice is on-device dictation feeding the same text path. Allergies, diet, deletes, billing and household membership are never visible to the model. The three moments change six things.

| # | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **`plan_week` widens from dinners to the full day.** It takes labels and kinds, calls the hybrid planner above, and stays risk 2 (preview, tap commits) | ADR-0013 free lists replace the dinner-only plan |
| 2 | **New tools:** `snap_dish(photo_ref)` (the same pipeline, returns a draft card), `add_food_item(day, text, label?)`, `label_item`, `adapt_recipe(recipe_id, brief)` (makes a fork; draft card, Save on tap), `log_portion` (only if F-6 says yes). The count goes from 17 to about 21 | The catalogue still sits far below the 20–50 tools where Shopify saw boundaries blur ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/building-production-ready-agentic-systems)) |
| 3 | **`search_recipes` gains real facets:** `max_minutes`, course and diet, applied in SQL. Allergen exclusion stays forced in SQL whatever the arguments say | "Show me 30-minute dinners" depends on R0.14 |
| 4 | **`put_in_plan` takes `label`, `position`, `kind` and `servings_planned`,** and stays one atomic `UPDATE` for replace | Plan items replace plan entries |
| 5 | **The context block adds a learned-taste summary** (top kept cuisines and proteins, recent swaps away from them) behind the stable prompt prefix | "Otto knows the person", without leaking allergies into model-editable state |
| 6 | **Moment ranking is updated.** The agent is moment 3, not moment 2. The "first kept Otto action" proxy stays as its activation measure | ADR-0013 |

Everything else stands: the six code prerequisites (P1 prefs on the account, P2 atomic swap, P3 server-side shopping list, P4 Postgres rate limiter, P5 `requireClub` always on, P6 consent on the account), the eval set with **zero allergen violations** as a release gate, and the cost estimate of $0.10–$1.00 per active user a month for text turns.

## The data model gains nine nouns, all additive

Today's model has good bones. Grams per line and USDA keys are in place, and `servings.count` is already the only divisor in the engine. But servings are a bare integer, or text on `favorites`. There is no yield or serving unit, no times, no image provenance, no fork lineage and no explicit plan order. Catalogue recipes cannot be edited at all: the Edit affordance renders only for `u-N` ids. Nutrition is a single point with a confidence label, where ADR-0013 promises `~` with a range. Every change below is additive. Most of them live in the canonical jsonb shape, which should become identical for catalogue and user recipes, plus a few small tables. This is the **recipe model v2** lane (discussion ticket **X2**).

| Change | Shape | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| **Yield and servings** in canonical jsonb | `yield {qty, unit: loaf\|pan\|pot\|batch…, cooked_total_g, basis}`; `servings {count, unit: slice\|piece\|bowl\|cookie…, unit_plural, units_per_serving, serving_g, basis: stated\|inferred\|photo\|user, confidence}` | Moment 1 serving units; "I ate 250 g" without hacks |
| **Per-line hints** | `scale: linear\|sublinear\|fixed\|to_taste`, `section`, `optional`, `confidence` | Honest scaling. Leavening and salt get a caution note, not a curve, because the sources disagree |
| **Times, equipment, facets** | `times {active, cook, passive, total}` with basis; `equipment[]`; `facets {course[], diet[], allergens[{tag, from[]}], cuisine, cost_band}` | Moment 2 filters; allergen explainability |
| **Nutrition with a range** | `per_100g`, `per_serving`, `range {low, high}`, `basis: computed_usda\|ai_estimate\|label\|user`, `reasons[]` | ADR-0013 `~`; "why is this an estimate" |
| **Origin, attribution, version** | `origin: catalogue\|import_url\|import_text\|photo_snap\|ai_generated\|ai_adapted\|manual\|fork`; `ai {model, generated_at}`; `attribution {}`; `version`; `parent {ref, version}` | Honesty, IP and forks |
| `recipe_versions(recipe_ref, version, canonical, author_kind, change_summary)` | New table | Last month's plan still shows what was cooked |
| `recipe_media(id, recipe_ref, kind: user_photo\|catalogue\|ai_generated, storage_path, digital_source_type, prompt_hash)` | New table; primary chosen by rule (user photo > catalogue > AI), never by a flag | ADR-0014. The in-app "AI image" tag is driven by `kind`, and the IPTC `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` tag is written into the file, since metadata can be stripped ([IPTC](https://iptc.org/news/iptc-publishes-metadata-guidance-for-ai-generated-synthetic-media/)) |
| `recipe_user_state(user_id, recipe_ref, default_servings, rating, notes, hidden, last_cooked_at)` | New overlay table, no fork | Personal data on catalogue recipes that survives catalogue fixes |
| `cook_events(id, user_id, household_id?, recipe_ref, recipe_version, servings_made, cooked_at, photo_media_id?)` | New table | Leftovers, North Star, "Snap your plate", learning |
| `plan_entries` → plan items | `+ position numeric, + label text null, + kind, + servings_planned, + recipe_version, + cook_event_id, + food_ref jsonb, + source otto\|user, + agent_action_id` | Free lists, full days, leftovers, undo |
| `plan_item_portions(plan_item_id, member_id, portions)` | Optional table (gate F-7) | Households: what to cook = sum of portions |
| `snaps(id, user_id, photo_media_id, recipe_ref, recipe_version, portion_count, portion_unit, portion_g, portion_basis, kcal_est, kcal_low, kcal_high, eaten_at, plan_item_id?)` | New table | "You photographed 2 slices", kept separate from the recipe |

Four rules hold the model together. A photo creates a **whole-yield recipe plus a snap row, never a "2 slices" recipe**. Catalogue rows are immutable per version, and the first content edit forks into `recipes` with a parent pointer; this is also what "Make my own" in moment 1, agent tweaks in moment 2 and "make this vegetarian and save it" in moment 3 all need. Every derived number keeps its basis and confidence. Values are stored at full precision and rounded only for display.

**Migrating existing users** happens in three layers. The server-side work is safe and additive. New columns get nulls or defaults: label null, `kind` derived from whether `recipe_id` is null, `position` backfilled from `created_at` order, and a serving unit of "serving". Existing `note = 'leftovers'` rows become `kind = leftover` without a cook-event link. Then come the old schema debts, best paid in the same window: convert the text `user_id` columns on favorites, recipes, plan_entries and shares to uuid foreign keys (after checking every value is a valid uid and cleaning orphans), and reconcile `favorites.recipe_id integer` with `plan_entries.recipe_id text`. Saved user-recipe nutrition is recomputed in a batch once the output schema gains ranges; the engine is deterministic, so this is safe. Device-only data (prefs, personal shopping state, consent, ratings, chats, journal photos as local file URIs) can only move through a **one-time client upload on first launch of the new version**. That upload runs only on phones that update and are online. Data on phones that never update is lost, so the release notes and the S9 copy should say so plainly. The live database may differ from the migrations (the storage policy may have been created by hand), so inspect it before writing any migration.

## Keep the platform, rebuild two pieces, add the new core

The assessment supports **"new core features on a kept platform"**. A literal rewrite would throw away the three most expensive and most tested assets: the nutrition engine with its golden corpus, whose pinned kcal ranges "are LAW"; the canonical catalogue; and the RLS model. It would gain nothing on any moment, because none of them is blocked by the shell, auth, purchases or UI kit. The blockers are missing data nouns, device-only state and a tool-less AI, and a rewrite would have to build exactly those anyway.

### Risks, and what not to do

The largest risk is **photo accuracy at the first impression**. Reconstruction quality on amounts and yield is unbenchmarked anywhere, and look-alike dishes and hidden fats are predictable misses. The mitigations are the confirm chips, the explicit-fat lines, the range display and the calibration gate. If F-2 cannot be met, ship Snap with a "closest Otto recipe" result as the primary answer and reconstruction as "Make my own", rather than shipping unmeasured numbers. **Pre-account abuse** is next: an open, AI-spending endpoint is exactly the problem `canonicalize` has today, so the scan endpoint must not exist before the durable limiter and attestation do. **Allergy safety** in plans depends on reviewed tags and server-side prefs. Until both land, the planner filters conservatively and the agent stays read-only on recipes. `food` items carry only a keyword check and must say so. **Migration loss** of device-only data is unavoidable for phones that never update, so be honest about it rather than promising continuity. **Cost drift** is real: per-scan costs look 2–20× higher than the memo assumes, and three free scans per device is an acquisition cost to model, not a rounding error. **App Review** exposure sits on 5.1.2(i) (consent before the pre-account upload) and 1.4.1 (methodology for health numbers). Both are design inputs, not afterthoughts.

What not to do follows from the evidence. Do not let the model write calories, choose allergen safety, or emit recipe ids outside the candidate list. Do not store a "2 slices" recipe. Do not rewrite the platform. Do not build MILP before there are budget or nutrient targets to optimise. Do not force calorie targets or show daily totals by default. Do not add web retrieval or self-consistency voting in 1.1: generate once, check in code, and regenerate only on failure. Do not ship the agent before the action catalogue test exists. Do not use a generated image for a snapped dish. Do not market accuracy.

## Conclusion

The founder's "as if from scratch" framing is right about the product and wrong about the code. The product really is new. Snap any dish produces a recipe nobody else produces: whole-batch, served in natural units, numbers by arithmetic, fat on the page. The planner treats the user's own cooking as first-class and the full day as a free list, and the agent makes every edit a sentence. But the code already holds the hardest-won pieces those moments stand on: deterministic nutrition, a grammed catalogue and review-first saving. The gap between today and the three moments is mostly *nouns*, not *engines*: yield, serving unit, portion, plan-item kind, cook event, fork and media provenance. That is why the single most consequential addition to the roadmap is a Foundations lane for recipe model v2, and why the single most consequential gate is a calibration set Otto builds itself.

Each moment also teaches the others. Every snap is a labelled example of what this person eats, every swap is a negative label for the planner, and every cook event is ground truth for the North Star. The data model should capture those signals from day one, even before the planner uses them. A loop that starts with an honest recipe and ends with a cooked one is a moat that photo-calorie apps cannot copy without rebuilding around recipes, and that recipe apps cannot copy without a deterministic nutrition engine.
