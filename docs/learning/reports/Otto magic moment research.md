# Make tonight's dinner Otto's magic moment
Status: draft · Last verified against code at commit 395191be · 2026-11-04 · Research synthesis; every "users love X" statement about Otto is a hypothesis, because the repo has no reviews, analytics or tester feedback

Otto's magic moment should be **"Tonight is answered"**: about a minute after first launch, the "Your first week" reveal opens on *tonight's* dinner, dated and sized for the user's household, with the rest of the week below it. Any day can be swapped with one tap, and a shopping list grouped by aisle is already written. Everything after that point (account, paywall, cooking, Otto AI) exists to keep that promise. The evidence supports this choice for three reasons. First, the pain is real and frequent: the nightly decision, plus being too tired at 6pm, is the only dinner pain that is daily, intense *and* already paid for (delivery at roughly $1,566–1,844 a year per user; meal kits). It hits **couples and parents cooking for two or more** hardest. Second, Otto's hard paywall makes the reveal its *only* chance to show value before the money ask, and **80–89% of subscription trials start on install day** ([RevenueCat 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions)). Third, the reveal runs on the phone from the `otto_recipes` catalogue with no AI and no account, so it is buildable today and honest by construction. Otto AI, which Juan calls the app's big feature, is better placed as the **hook that keeps the plan alive after purchase** ("Otto, swap Thursday, I've got chicken thighs") than as the first impression: AI apps retain annual subscribers worse than non-AI apps, and AI consent plus an account would have to come before any pre-paywall AI moment. The activation event to measure is **"cooked at least one planned dinner within the 7-day trial"**. The habit is weekly (plan, shop, cook), not daily. Otto can measure all of this without a third-party SDK, using its own Supabase tables plus RevenueCat cohorts, provided `APP_PRIVACY_TRUTH_TABLE.md` is updated first.

## An aha is a feeling; activation is the number that stands in for it

People use "aha moment", "magic moment" and "activation" as if they meant the same thing. They don't, and a founder who confuses them will optimise the wrong screen. Lenny Rachitsky defines the activation milestone as "the earliest point in your onboarding flow that, by showing your product's value, is predictive of long-term retention" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-activation-rate)). Amplitude frames the aha as the first time a user understands the core value, and warns that "a smooth onboarding experience is important, but it's not the aha moment itself" ([Amplitude](https://www.amplitude.com/blog/aha-moment)). The working vocabulary is a chain. The **promise** is the value proposition. The **setup moment** is the minimum input needed to reach value. The **aha or magic moment** is the felt payoff, described in words. The **activation event** is a measurable proxy for the aha, written as "action × threshold × time window". The **habit moment** is the core action repeated at the product's natural frequency ([Amplitude Community](https://community.amplitude.com/discussion/12791/the-4-stages-of-activation); [PLG activation guide](https://skills.sh/skenetechnologies/plg-skills/activation-metrics)). The **North Star** is value delivered across all users at steady state. Activation is a leading indicator of the North Star, not the North Star itself. Chamath Palihapitiya's original Facebook framing was simpler: get people in the door, get them to the aha as fast as possible, deliver core value as often as possible ([Recapio digest](https://recapio.com/digest/chamath-palihapitiya-how-we-put-facebook-on-the-path-to-1-billion-users-by-gagan-biyani)). No source attributes the "setup/habit moment" words to him.

The famous activation numbers are all the same shape, and all were found by looking backwards. Facebook's "7 friends in 10 days" was a correlation with retention, used as an onboarding *target* through friend suggestions, not proof of cause ([PM case study](https://scratch-puffin-363.notion.site/Facebook-s-7-friends-activation-story-a-PM-case-study-33dec508b5118053bff8e0233fedd0f3)). At Twitter, Josh Elman found that users who followed about 30 accounts stayed, so the team rebuilt first-run around interest-based follow suggestions ([Mattermark](https://mattermark.com/putting-it-all-together-how-josh-elman-identified-a-growth-driver-at-twitter/)). Slack's "2,000 messages" has no primary transcript and should be treated as folklore. The best documented consumer case is Duolingo. Its streak work came from the observation that users with a 10-day streak rarely dropped off, and a "streak saver" notification was the first big win. The share of DAU with 7+ day streaks rose almost 3x, and DAU rose 4.5x over four years, from many combined efforts ([Lenny's Newsletter, Mazal](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). The lesson for Otto is to never borrow another app's threshold. Otto's own numbers have to come from Otto's own cohorts.

Finding the moment has three steps. Brainstorm candidate milestones. Test which ones separate retained from churned users. Then run an experiment that pushes more users through the milestone, to prove cause ([Lenny, via mirror](https://personal-learnings.pages.dev/reading_room/issues/lennys_newsletter/0440_how-to-determine-your-activation-metric.html)). The regression step needs enough retained *and* churned users in each cohort to mean anything. Otto has neither data nor volume yet, so for now the work is hypothesis, qualitative observation and experiments with large effect sizes. The **Sean Ellis question** ("How would you feel if you could no longer use Otto?") sets the bar: under 40% "very disappointed" usually means no product-market fit ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/product-market-fit-survey)). Superhuman turned that survey into a roadmap by segmenting the people who answered "very disappointed", and reportedly moved from 22% to 58% ([Superhuman](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)). The 40% line is a correlation from Ellis's own portfolio, not a law ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/startup-fundamentals/sean-ellis-test)).

Design research adds four constraints. The Fogg model says behaviour needs motivation, ability and a prompt at the same moment ([behaviormodel.org](https://behaviormodel.org/)). Nir Eyal's Hook model adds an *investment* step that "loads the next trigger" ([Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/hooked/)). Things people build themselves are overvalued (the IKEA effect), but only if they finish them ([Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-2473-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)). Habits take time. In Lally et al.'s study, automaticity peaked at a median of about **66 days, ranging from 18 to a modelled 254**, and a single missed day did not break the habit ([UCL](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit)). A 7-day trial therefore cannot form a habit. It can only deliver the aha and make the habit believable.

## Every reference app shows a personal result before it asks for anything

The seven reference apps differ widely in length and aggression, but they share one rule: the user sees something that is visibly *theirs* before the account or the money. Duolingo puts a real lesson and a placement result before sign-up. Its growth team said that "simply moving the sign-up screen back a few steps led to about a 20% increase in DAUs" ([Taplytics, quoting Gotthilf](https://taplytics.com/blog/duolingo-ab-test-onboarding)). There is no onboarding paywall, and the streak is the retention engine: 600+ streak A/B tests, about half of them failures, and called "our biggest growth lever" by its retention PM ([Lenny's Podcast](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)). Learners on a 7-day streak are 3.6x more likely to finish a course ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)), though that is an observational comparison. The flexibility features worked best. Decoupling the streak from the daily XP goal, so that any one lesson counts, raised learners on a 7+ day streak by 40% ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Duolingo also admitted in 2025 that extra monetisation friction was part of why DAU growth slowed ([Duolingo investor materials](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/961ce633-3cee-49d0-bd7a-2c63731d45fb)).

Cal AI is the opposite extreme and the closest structural match to Otto's hard paywall. It runs about 30 investment questions, a "generating plan" screen, a personalised plan reveal, and then a hard paywall. The real magic moment, photo to calories, only comes after payment ([local flow study](../../flow-maps/research/02_REFERENCE_APP_FLOWS.md)). Its founder says extra questions raised conversion because users invest time ([Founderpath](https://founderpath.com/resources/zach-yadegari-cal-ai-onboarding-tests-and-influencer-marketing)). Superwall, a vendor, reports **trial-to-paid +31% across 123 experiments** ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/case-studies/cal-ai)). The model has a boundary. In April 2026 Apple briefly pulled Cal AI under Guidelines 3.1.1, 3.1.2(c) and 5.6, partly because its paywall showed a weekly price more prominently than the amount actually billed ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/04/21/popular-calorie-tracker-briefly-pulled-from-app-store-over-iap-and-billing-violations/)). MyFitnessPal, which bought Cal AI in March 2026, has the computed calorie goal as its first value and daily logging as its habit. When it moved barcode scanning behind Premium in 2022, reviews "tanked" ([Droid Life](https://www.droid-life.com/2022/08/24/myfitnesspal-puts-barcode-scanner-behind-premium-paywall/)). Users experience the fastest path to the core action as the product itself.

The calmer apps show the same rule with less pressure. Tiimo asks only two questions, and its click is identity plus visuals: *my* day, laid out calmly, in "the only one that has actually helped" ([AppFollow reviews](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/tiimo-to-do-list-planner/1480220328?country=us)). It was Apple's iPhone App of the Year 2025 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/tiimo/id1480220328)). Headspace's moment is the first finished meditation. Deep-linked pushes toward that first session raised first-meditation starts by 14% and week-1 retention by 109% ([Phiture](https://phiture.com/success-stories/headspace-retention/)). But a quiz-based onboarding redesign that doubled course starts left users who "still didn't meditate more" ([Irrational Labs](https://irrationallabs.com/case-studies/headspace-doubled-course-starts/)). That is the clearest warning in the set that a start is not a habit. Yazio follows the questionnaire, plan, paywall pattern, with discount gimmicks, and uses a free fasting countdown as its reason to return. Its data is thin and mostly third-party ([NutriScan](https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/yazio-pricing-2026-free-vs-pro-what-pro-unlocks-33b26f8fc7)).

Cherrypick is Otto's nearest analogue. Its promise is "Your meals & groceries, sorted" ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/cherrypick-plan-shop-cook/id1567423761)). Its moment has two beats, "Your meal matches are ready" and then a real Tesco or Sainsbury's basket, and both come before any money ask. Its cadence is weekly, and it serves 330,000+ households ([The Grocer](https://thegrocer.co.uk/news/meal-planning-and-shopping-platform-lollipop-rebrands-as-cherrypick/687537.article)). Its founder says grocery margins were "very tight and insufficient to cover costs", which pushed it to add paid tiers ([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/online/shopping-platform-cherrypick-to-introduce-paid-for-tiers/692727.article)). Otto cannot copy the basket: it has no grocer integration. Otto's equivalent second beat is the shopping list that is already written.

| App | Magic moment | Before the wall? | Return hook | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Duolingo | First lesson + placement | Yes, no onboarding paywall | Forgiving streak, streak pushes | Daily |
| Cal AI | Plan reveal (proxy); photo scan after paying | Reveal yes, core action no | Logging rings | Daily |
| MyFitnessPal | Calorie goal | Yes | Food log against goal | Daily |
| Tiimo | My day, visual | After account, before soft paywall | Visual Today | Daily |
| Headspace | First meditation | After paywall | Streak after session | Daily |
| Cherrypick | Matches ready, then basket | Yes, no onboarding paywall | Plan + shop | **Weekly** |
| Yazio | Plan reveal, fasting timer | Unverified | Fasting countdown | Several times a day |

### Recipe competitors stop at "saved", "listed" or "generated"

The recipe market splits into camps, and each camp's magic moment turns intent into an artifact in seconds. *Keepers* win on import. Paprika's loyalty rests on "Paprika just works" and parsing that "is amazing" ([Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/16213756-paprika-recipe-manager-app-40-50-off-windows-mac-android-ios)). ReciMe, Pestle and Honeydew all lead with "share a reel, get a recipe" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/25/pestle-recipe-app-can-now-save-dishes-from-tiktok)). That is also the category's most complained-about failure: ReciMe reviews say "95% of the recipes on TikTok DO NOT download" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1593779280)), and accuracy collapses when the recipe lives only in the audio ([WhistleOut](https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/Apps/recime-app-review)). *Planners* win on plan-to-list. Mealime auto-generates the grocery list from chosen meals ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/best-meal-planning-apps/)). Plan to Eat is a drag-to-calendar planner that ships no recipes ([Plan to Eat FAQ](https://learn.plantoeat.com/help/frequently-asked-questions)). Ollie sells "Healthy Eating, Without the Mental Load" at $9.99/mo to 50,000+ families ([Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/food/ai-apps-meal-planning-shopping-20250828.html)). *Kits* win on "dinner shows up", but HelloFresh active customers fell from 7.1M to 6.6M (2023), and the company says meal-quality issues hurt retention in 2025 ([RetailDetail](https://www.retaildetail.eu/news/food/hellofresh-earns-more-from-fewer-customers); [HelloFresh annual report](https://financialreports.eu/filings/hellofresh-se/management-reports/2026/32970107/)). Yummly, the closest "everything" app, was shut down by Whirlpool in December 2024, and users lost their collections ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yummly)).

Two retention findings matter most for Otto. Generative-AI apps had a median one-month retention of about **42% against 63%** for incumbent consumer categories ([Voicebot on Sequoia data](https://voicebot.ai/2023/10/03/generative-ai-apps-struggle-with-retention-and-engagement-charts/)). RevenueCat's 2026 data, cited secondhand, puts annual-subscriber retention at **21.1% for AI apps against 30.7% for non-AI apps** ([DEV summary](https://dev.to/zaraai_0b75675ddc9204c716/ai-apps-generate-41-more-revenue-per-user-but-lose-79-of-annual-subscribers-before-month-13-485a)). a16z advises rebasing AI retention to Month 3, because "AI tourists" distort early curves ([a16z](https://a16z.com/ai-retention-benchmarks/)). Separately, people "save far more recipes than they actually cook", and *weeknight fit* is what turns a save into a meal ([FishDog](https://fishdog-web.pages.dev/research-studies/cooking-content-weeknight-applicability-drives-conversion)). Utility loops tied to the week (plan, list, cook) and a growing personal library drive retention. Generation on its own is novelty.

## "What's for dinner" is a daily, paid-for pain, sharpest for households of two or more

The problem is large for a reason no survey has to prove: the decision comes around about seven times a week, every week. The intensity numbers are real but mostly vendor-funded, and should be read that way. In a Factor/Wakefield survey of 1,000 US adults in couples, **68% said deciding what to eat is their biggest meal-time challenge, 44% struggle to agree on a plan most nights, and 25% argue about dinner once or twice a week** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250127681988/en)). A Hopscotch/OnePoll survey found more than half of US adults cook the same meals week after week, rising to 65% of Gen Z. The AP calls this "dinner fatigue" ([AP via Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/sep/29/dinner-fatigue-fix-planning/)). About 8 in 10 say they are too tired to cook after work ([WTVY, Kevin's/Talker](https://www.wtvy.com/2024/08/31/nearly-80-americans-are-too-tired-cook-after-work-research-says)). The neutral time data is tight. The BLS American Time Use Survey puts food preparation and cleanup at about 40 minutes a day on average: about 52 for women and 28 for men ([BLS ATUS](https://www.bls.gov/charts/american-time-use/activity-by-hldh.htm)). A weeknight recipe therefore has to fit in about 30 minutes to be realistic. That last figure is an inference, not a measurement.

Money is the motivation people already act on. Food spending rose about 15% per person between January 2022 and September 2024 ([Purdue CFDAS](https://ag.purdue.edu/cfdas/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Report_202409-2.pdf)). Delivery costs users roughly **$1,566–1,844 a year** ([Upgraded Points](https://upgradedpoints.com/news/americans-food-delivery-habits); [Circuit](https://getcircuit.com/teams/blog/hidden-cost-of-delivery)), and 89% say cooking at home is one of the best ways to save ([Flashfood/Harris](https://stocks.observer-reporter.com/observerreporter/article/bizwire-2025-1-14-survey-reveals-majority-of-americans-plan-to-cook-at-home-more-in-2025-to-save-money)). Against that, Otto's $39.99 a year is less than two delivery orders. That comparison is a framing for the founder, not paywall copy. Food waste is real: the EPA puts it at about $728 per person per year ([EPA](https://epa.gov/land-research/estimating-cost-food-waste-american-consumers)). But it is felt as secondary, since 64% of UK households think some waste is "inevitable" ([WRAP](https://wrap.ngo/resources/report/understanding-household-food-waste-uk-household-food-management-survey)). The saved-recipe graveyard is a good story with no hard numbers behind it.

The pain is not evenly spread. **Couples** negotiate dinner nightly (the Factor data). **Parents** cook around picky eaters: 45% of UK parents cook more than one meal a night ([HelloFresh UK](https://blog.hellofresh.co.uk/cooking-for-fussy-eaters/)). And the *mental load* of anticipating and monitoring dinner falls mostly on women, even when their partners do the cooking ([Daminger, via UW–Madison](https://ls.wisc.edu/news/how-invisible-labor-affects-relationships)). That load is linked to stress and burnout in mothers of young children ([USC](https://publicexchange.usc.edu/?p=4938)). Mixed diets compound it, since 57% of Americans now follow a named eating pattern ([IFIC via FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/01/21/ific-survey-how-americans-view-health-and-food-choices/)). Singles feel the money and waste pain but little of the coordination pain. Otto's best-fit customer is therefore **the person in a household of two or more who carries the dinner decision**. The first onboarding question ("Who's eating") already sorts users by that.

One finding should shape the design directly. WRAP names "displaced meals" (planned meals that don't happen) as a main driver of waste ([WRAP](https://wrap.ngo/resources/report/understanding-household-food-waste-uk-household-food-management-survey)), and meal kits lose customers to rigidity. A fixed 7-day plan is a weaker promise than *a plan that bends*. Otto's magic moment has to be about tonight first, with a week that can change, not about a perfect week.

## Otto's proposal: one magic moment, one hook, one promise

### What Otto is selling, what people get hooked on, and the moment that proves it

These are three different things, and the current store copy blurs them. The listing leads with a mood ("A quieter kind of cookbook"), and its promotional text reads like a feature list ([STORE_METADATA.md](../../release/STORE_METADATA.md)). Using April Dunford's order, which starts from the alternatives because "that's how customers think" ([Heinz Marketing summary](https://www.heinzmarketing.com/blog/five-components-of-effective-positioning-an-obviously-awesome-book-summary-part-2/)), Otto's positioning works out as follows. **Alternatives:** the same five dinners on rotation, TikTok saves plus a notes app, delivery, meal kits. **Unique attributes:** a dated week built for who's eating, swappable in one tap, from a curated catalogue with credited sources and honest on-device nutrition; a shopping list grouped by aisle that syncs live across the household ([useHousehold.ts](../../../src/features/household/useHousehold.ts)); any link, caption or photo imported straight into that week; and Otto AI to rearrange the plan in plain words. **Value:** tonight is decided, the shop is written, and nobody in the house has to carry it. **Target:** households of two or more who cook most weeknights. **Category:** a dinner planner, *not* a recipe keeper. The keeper category solves the low-urgency graveyard pain and competes with one-time-purchase Paprika.

The **selling point** is "dinner decided for the week, for your household". The **feature people get hooked on** is predicted to be the *Tonight* card together with Otto AI as the plan's repair tool. When life breaks Wednesday's plan, the user tells Otto what's in the fridge and gets a credited recipe dropped into that slot, with the list updated. This is how to honour Juan's view that talking with Otto is the big feature ([ADR-0008](../../adr/0008-tab-bar-home-plan-cookbook-account-with-otto-ai-in-the-trail.md)) without staking conversion on AI novelty. Chat that resolves into a plan slot is a utility loop. Chat that invents recipes for fun is a tourist attraction. Both points are hypotheses, because nothing in the repo shows what Otto's users love ([otto_assets notes](../research_notes/Otto%20magic%20moment%20research/otto_assets.md)).

The **magic moment** is the reveal, described next.

### The exact moment and the screens that lead to it

The current redesigned flow is: launch screen → Welcome → three questions (Who's eating · How do you eat, including allergies · Anything to skip), each with Skip → **Your first week** → Save your plan (account, Apple first) → Otto Club paywall (no close) → App Store sheet → You're all set → dinner reminders primer → tabs ([otto-next.html](../../flow-maps/maps/otto-next.html)). This structure is right, and removing the taste swipe and the "Building" screen was correct. The swipe added time and, as Headspace shows, quizzes inflate starts rather than habits. A "Building" animation would claim work that the phone does in milliseconds. One piece of doc drift needs fixing: `otto-next.html`'s intro paragraph (line 108) states the current flow, but its summary strip, comparison table, scenario row and reminders row (lines 121, 146, 192, 217) still show Taste, Building and reminders *before* the paywall.

The proposal changes nothing in the order. It sharpens what the reveal does:

| Step | Screen | What happens | Target time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Launch → Welcome | Painted Otto, one line of promise, one button | ~5 s |
| 1–3 | Three questions, each with Skip | Every answer must visibly change the reveal: servings from "Who's eating", hard filters from allergies and "skip" | ~10–15 s each |
| 4 | **Your first week: the magic moment** | Opens on **Tonight** (after about 8pm, on **Tomorrow**): one large dated card with photo, time, servings for the household, credited source. Below it, the next six dates as smaller cards. **Swap** on every card draws the next-best match from `otto_recipes`. A **list preview** ("Your list: N items, grouped by aisle") opens a read-only list sheet. No AI, no network beyond the public catalogue read | 30–60 s of play |
| 5 | Save your plan | Account, Apple first, framed as keeping *this* week | ~10 s |
| 6 | Otto Club (no close) | Restates their week (tonight's photo, the 7 dates) above the offer | — |
| 7 | App Store sheet → You're all set | "Tonight: {dish}." One button: *Start cooking* or *See your week* | — |
| 8 | Dinner reminders primer | "Otto can nudge you at {time} on cooking nights." Not now allowed | — |

The target is a **median under 90 seconds from first open to the reveal**. That is a hypothesis to measure, not a claim to print. The swap is the most important detail. It turns a generated artifact into something the user shaped (investment and the IKEA effect), it shows on the first screen that the plan bends (the answer to WRAP's displaced meals), and it costs no AI. The list preview is the second beat, the way Cherrypick's basket is: proof that the week turns into errands done. Two engineering checks come before committing. First, the store-grouped list logic must be able to run on an unsaved, anonymous plan held in memory; the README describes the list, but this note did not confirm that it can run detached from an account. Second, the catalogue must have enough recipes per filter combination that "swap" never runs dry. If a strict allergy filter leaves too few, the card should say so plainly, never pad.

### Where the paywall sits, and how to make it honest and persuasive

The paywall sits **directly after the magic moment and the account**, which is Cal AI's position, with Duolingo's lesson that value comes before the wall. This is the only defensible placement under [ADR-0005](../../adr/0005-hard-paywall-an-account-and-an-active-otto-club-are-required.md). Hard paywalls convert much better than freemium: a median download-to-paid D35 of about **12.1% against 2.2% (2025)**, or about 10.7% against 2.1% in 2026 coverage ([RevenueCat 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions)). Because nearly all trials start on Day 0, a moment that only arrives on Day 3 misses the decision. Seven-day trials are cancelled on Day 0 far less often than 3-day trials (31% against 55%+), which supports Otto's 7-day choice ([Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/paywall-conversion-structural-decisions)). No study isolates whether a pre-paywall aha raises trial-to-paid, so Otto has to test it.

The paywall should restate *their* week, not generic benefits. The roadmap already lists the missing one-line benefit and the undated trial terms ([REWORK_ROADMAP.md](../../tickets/REWORK_ROADMAP.md)). Under **Guideline 3.1.2(c), the amount billed must be the most prominent price**: "$39.99 a year after your 7-day free trial" is the headline, and $4.99 a month is the alternative. A per-week or per-month equivalent of the annual plan must not appear larger than the billed amount, or at all, given Cal AI's removal. A dated trial timeline ("Today: your week is ready · {date}: trial ends, $39.99 billed") is honest and reduces anxiety. "Otto reminds you 2 days before" may only appear once the planned trial-ending reminder (row `trialrem` in otto-next.html) is actually built and permitted. Otherwise it is a dead promise.

### The habit loop after the moment

Meal planning's natural frequency is **weekly with a daily beat**, so Otto should not import Duolingo's daily streak. The loop, in Hook terms:

| Hook step | Otto version | Exists today? |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger (daily) | Cooking-night reminder: "Tonight in your kitchen · {dish}. Ready to start cooking?" | Yes ([notifications.logic.ts](../../../src/features/notifications/notifications.logic.ts)) |
| Trigger (weekly) | "Plan your week · A fresh week ahead." on the user's chosen day | Yes, same file |
| Action | Home's Tonight card → cook mode, or *Swap* / *Ask Otto* if plans changed | Cook mode yes; Tonight-first Home is in Juan's idea list |
| Reward | "Dinner, done. Otto's proud of you." with proud otter and sound; the partner's phone checks off the list live | Yes ([CookScreen.tsx](../../../src/features/cook/CookScreen.tsx)) |
| Variable reward | Next week's plan brings new matches; Otto's answer to "what can I make with…" | Partly |
| Investment | Imports go into the Cookbook *and* into next week's candidates; "cooked it" and "not again" teach the plan; inviting a household member | Import and household yes; feedback-to-plan no |

Two additions matter most. First, a one-tap **"Cooked it"** on the Tonight card, because most people won't run cook mode for a dish they know, and the activation metric must not miss them. Second, a quiet **weekly count** ("4 dinners cooked this week") in place of a loss-aversion streak. If a streak is ever added, Duolingo's evidence says the unit should be small (one planned dinner cooked in a week counts) and forgiving (freezes). That fits Otto's "Open. No plans, no guilt." voice better than guilt pushes ([05_BRAND_AND_VOICE.md](../../redesign-2026-11/docs/05_BRAND_AND_VOICE.md)). The household is the strongest lock-in: a second person who relies on the shared list is a reason to renew that no AI feature matches. "Invite whoever shops with you" belongs on Day 1–3, after the first cook, not in onboarding.

### The promise and positioning in one sentence

**Promise (store subtitle candidate):** "Tonight's dinner, and the week's, decided." **Positioning sentence:** "For households tired of deciding dinner every night, Otto plans the week from honest, credited recipes and writes the shopping list, and when plans change, you just tell Otto." Both obey the honesty laws: no user counts, no recipe count (the store rules ban one), no "AI meal planning" (the plan isn't AI, and the store rules forbid the claim), no invented precision. Do not write "in 60 seconds" until Otto's own median proves it. The share line in the brand doc ("the easiest way to cook") is an unprovable superlative and should be retired.

### Rejected candidates

| Candidate | Why it loses to "Tonight is answered" |
|---|---|
| Otto AI chat as the first moment | Needs named AI consent ([ADR-0006](../../adr/0006-ai-runs-on-openai-behind-supabase-edge-functions-with-per-pr.md)) and, in practice, an account before any call. It is non-deterministic in the one screen that must not fail, it costs money per anonymous user, and AI-first retention is weaker. It is kept as the post-purchase hook |
| Import a reel or link into a clean recipe | Requires the user to have a link ready during onboarding, and it is the category's most visible failure. It solves "where did I save that", not dinner. It is the best Day-1 moment for keepers |
| Cook-mode finish, "Dinner, done" | The most emotional beat Otto has, but it comes after the paywall and takes 30+ minutes. It becomes the **activation event** |
| Shared list updating on another phone | Technically the most magical, but needs a second person. It is the Day-2-to-7 household moment and the strongest investment |
| Taste swipe, "matches ready" | Already removed. It adds time, and quiz-style steps inflate starts without lifting habit (Headspace) |
| "Building your plan" animation | Removed, correctly. It implies work that isn't happening, which breaks the honesty laws |
| Nutrition or calorie plan reveal (Cal AI style) | Wrong job for Otto's customer. Otto's nutrition is an honest estimate (`~`), not a target engine |
| Fridge photo to tonight's recipe | Good future Otto AI feature, but it is AI, needs consent, and accuracy is unproven. A post-purchase experiment |

### How to measure it with no analytics SDK

Otto can measure the whole funnel with tools it already has. **RevenueCat** gives trial starts, trial-to-paid and renewal by cohort, which covers conversion. **Supabase** already stores the behavioural truth: `plan_entries` with dates, cooked journal entries and household membership. For the gaps, add a small first-party `app_events` table: insert-only under RLS, object-action names, no third party. Pre-account onboarding steps are buffered on the phone and written after "Save your plan". Anonymous drop-off before the account can be counted as aggregate step counters with no identifier, or left to App Store Connect's opt-in App Analytics. Any new collection must be reflected in [APP_PRIVACY_TRUTH_TABLE.md](../../legal/APP_PRIVACY_TRUTH_TABLE.md) before the build ships. This becomes the tracking plan and North Star tree that the earlier artifacts guide found missing ([App documentation artifacts guide](App%20documentation%20artifacts%20guide.md)).

| Layer | Metric | Source | First target (hypothesis) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to value | Median seconds, first open → reveal shown | `onboarding_step` events, flushed after account | < 90 s |
| Moment engagement | % of reveals with ≥1 swap or list open | events | Baseline first |
| Conversion | Reveal → account → trial start | events + RevenueCat | Beat the current flow's install → trial |
| **Activation** | **≥1 planned dinner cooked (cook-mode finish or "Cooked it") within 7 days of trial start** | journal / plan tables | Find the threshold that best separates renewers |
| Conversion outcome | Trial → paid | RevenueCat | Compare activated vs not |
| Habit | Next week planned by day 8; ≥2 planned dinners cooked in week 2 | `plan_entries`, journal | — |
| **North Star** | **Planned dinners cooked per active Club household per week** | tables | — |
| AI guardrail | Share of plan changes made through Otto AI; AI users' retention rebased to Month 3 | events | — |

The activation threshold ("≥1 in 7 days") is a starting guess. Once a few hundred trials have resolved, compare 1, 2 and 3 cooked dinners and pick the one that best separates paid from churned, then push users toward it.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question Otto should be asking. Otto doesn't need a more impressive feature. It needs to answer one question, "what's for dinner tonight?", inside the single minute the hard paywall gives it, and then keep answering it every night for a week. That reframes Otto AI. It is not the wow that sells the trial; it is the reason the plan survives contact with real life, which is where meal kits and rigid planners lose people. It also reframes the paywall. The paywall shouldn't sell features; it should offer to keep this specific week. The biggest risk is not choosing the wrong moment. It is shipping the new flow without the events table, and learning nothing. ADR-0009 should be accepted, because the whole strategy depends on value coming before the account.

**Experiment plan, in order.** (1) Before the new flow ships, add `app_events` and the privacy table update, and record a two-week baseline of install → trial on the current flow. (2) Run five to eight moderated first-run sessions with Otto Insiders on TestFlight, aiming at couples and parents. Time the path to the reveal, watch whether people swap, and ask "what would you cook tonight?". Pair this with JTBD interviews about last week's actual dinners. (3) Ship the reveal v1 (tonight-first, swap, list preview) and compare install → trial against the baseline. Expect a before/after comparison, not significance, at Otto's volume, so act only on large effects. (4) A/B the paywall that restates the user's week against a generic one, with the billed amount most prominent in both. (5) Test the cooking-night reminder against a holdout, measured on activation. (6) Add an "Ask Otto to change this day" entry on plan cards and measure plan repairs and Month-3 retention. (7) From Day 14 to 30 of paid use, run the Sean Ellis survey segmented by household type, and let the "very disappointed" segment decide who Otto is for.
